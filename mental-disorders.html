EXPLORE

What is a mental disorder?

Mental disorders are health conditions involving significant changes in thinking, emotions, behaviour or functioning. They can affect people differently and may arise from interacting biological, psychological, social and environmental influences.

01

Conditions, not identities

A diagnosis describes a health condition. It does not describe the whole person, their abilities, their future or their worth.

02

Experiences vary

Two people with the same diagnosis may have very different experiences, strengths, needs and pathways to recovery.

03

Support can help

Early recognition, supportive relationships and appropriate professional care can make an important difference.

LEARN

Common Mental Health Conditions

The goal is not to memorize diagnostic criteria. The goal is to understand the broad nature of these conditions and to replace stereotypes with evidence.

A

Anxiety Disorders

Anxiety is a normal human response to perceived threat. Anxiety disorders involve excessive or persistent anxiety that can interfere with daily life.

D

Depressive Disorders

Depressive disorders can involve persistent low mood, reduced interest or pleasure, changes in energy and other symptoms that affect everyday functioning.

B

Bipolar Disorder

Bipolar disorder involves episodes of significant changes in mood, energy and activity, including periods of elevated or unusually activated mood and periods of depression.

P

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder

PTSD can develop after exposure to traumatic events and may involve persistent trauma-related symptoms that affect emotions, thoughts, sleep or everyday functioning.

O

Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

OCD involves persistent unwanted thoughts, images or urges and/or repetitive behaviours or mental acts that can become difficult to control and interfere with life.

E

Eating Disorders

Eating disorders are serious mental and physical health conditions involving disturbed relationships with eating, food, body image or related behaviours. They are not simply matters of appearance or willpower.

N

Neurodevelopmental Conditions

Neurodevelopmental conditions begin during development and can affect attention, learning, communication, behaviour or sensory processing. ADHD and autism are examples.

S

Psychotic Disorders

Psychotic disorders can affect perception, thinking and interpretation of reality. Early assessment and appropriate support are important.

THINK

Important distinctions

Normal emotion ≠ disorder

Everyone experiences sadness, worry, frustration or stress. A mental disorder involves a pattern of symptoms and difficulties that require appropriate clinical assessment.

Screening ≠ diagnosis

A questionnaire may identify symptoms that deserve further attention. It cannot by itself establish a diagnosis.

Diagnosis ≠ destiny

A diagnosis does not determine a person's abilities, relationships, education, career or future.

BREAK THE STIGMA

Mental illness should never become a label for a person.

Stigma can create shame, discrimination and social isolation. It can also discourage people from seeking appropriate support.

We can challenge stigma by using respectful language, learning from evidence, listening to lived experience and treating every person with dignity.

Listen Respect people's experiences.

Mental Disorders from SELF → WORLD

Mental health conditions are experienced by individuals, but their prevention, recognition and support are influenced by wider environments.

01

SELF

Symptoms, coping, strengths, health behaviours and personal experiences.

02

FAMILY & PEERS

Relationships, communication, bullying, belonging and social support.

03

COMMUNITY

Schools, recreation, safety, services and inclusive community environments.

04

COUNTRY

Health systems, education, social protection, policy and equitable access to care.

05

WORLD

Conflict, displacement, climate-related pressures, humanitarian crises and global inequalities.

EQUITY & SDH

Who has the greatest risks—and who has the greatest access to support?

Mental health is shaped partly by social determinants of health. Poverty, discrimination, violence, unsafe housing, exclusion, educational opportunities and access to services can influence both risk and recovery.

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

10 Questions — Understanding Mental Disorders

1. Which statement best describes a mental disorder?

Correct answer: B. A mental disorder is a health condition involving significant changes in thoughts, emotions, behaviour or functioning.

2. Which statement is most accurate about anxiety?

Correct answer: C. Anxiety is a normal human response, but anxiety disorders involve persistent or excessive anxiety that can interfere with daily life.

3. What is one important difference between screening and diagnosis?

Correct answer: A. Screening can identify symptoms that may warrant further assessment; diagnosis requires appropriate clinical evaluation.

4. Which statement about bipolar disorder is correct?

Correct answer: D. Bipolar disorder involves significant episodes of mood, energy and activity changes.

5. Which statement about eating disorders is most appropriate?

Correct answer: B. Eating disorders are serious health conditions and should not be reduced to appearance, weight or willpower.

6. Why is stigma harmful?

Correct answer: C. Stigma can contribute to discrimination, isolation and reluctance to seek appropriate support.

7. Which is an example of an equity question?

Correct answer: A. Asking who faces greater barriers to mental-health services is an equity-oriented question.

8. Which statement about neurodevelopmental conditions is correct?

Correct answer: D. They are associated with differences in development that can affect areas such as attention, communication, learning or sensory processing.

9. What is one effective way to challenge stigma?

Correct answer: B. Use respectful language, learn from evidence and avoid stereotypes.

10. Does a mental-health diagnosis define a person's identity?

Correct answer: A. No. A diagnosis describes a health condition; it does not define the whole person.
