Understanding Mental Health Disorders

Mental health disorders are real health conditions that can affect thoughts, emotions, behaviour, relationships and everyday life. Understanding them can reduce stigma, encourage early support and help us treat people with dignity and respect.

Remember: learning about a disorder does not mean diagnosing yourself or someone else.

WHY IS THIS IMPORTANT?

Mental health is part of health.

Everyone has mental health. People can experience changes in mood, thoughts, behaviour and wellbeing throughout life. Sometimes these experiences become persistent, severe or disruptive enough to require professional assessment and support.

Mental health disorders are health conditions—not signs of weakness, bad character or a lack of effort.

Learning about them helps us recognize concerns earlier, understand other people's experiences and create communities where seeking help is accepted rather than stigmatized.

THINK CRITICALLY

Feeling something is not the same as having a disorder.

Everyone experiences difficult emotions. A mental health disorder involves a pattern of symptoms or difficulties that deserves professional assessment when it significantly affects wellbeing or everyday functioning.

EVERYDAY EXPERIENCES

Normal emotional responses

  • Feeling nervous before an important event
  • Feeling sad after a disappointment
  • Being stressed during a difficult period
  • Having occasional difficulty sleeping
  • Feeling frustrated or overwhelmed sometimes
WHEN SUPPORT MAY BE NEEDED

Persistent or significant difficulties

  • Symptoms persist or repeatedly return
  • Daily activities become difficult
  • School, work or relationships are affected
  • Distress becomes difficult to manage
  • A qualified professional can assess what is happening
LEARN

Eight important groups to understand

These are broad groups rather than labels for people. A person's experience is always more complex than a diagnosis.

01

Anxiety Disorders

Anxiety is a normal response to uncertainty or perceived danger. Anxiety disorders involve excessive or persistent fear, worry or avoidance that can interfere with everyday life.

Key idea: the alarm system may become too frequent, intense or difficult to switch off.
02

Depressive Disorders

Depression involves persistent changes in mood, interest, energy, thinking and functioning. It is more than simply having a bad day.

Key idea: persistent low mood or loss of interest deserves attention and support.
03

Bipolar Disorder

Bipolar disorder involves episodes of significant changes in mood, energy and activity, including periods of elevated or unusually activated mood and periods of depression.

Key idea: these changes are more substantial than ordinary day-to-day mood variation.
04

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder

PTSD can develop after exposure to traumatic or extremely threatening experiences. People may experience persistent distress, unwanted memories, avoidance or changes in mood and alertness.

Key idea: trauma can affect how the brain and body respond to reminders of danger.
05

Eating Disorders

Eating disorders involve significant disturbances in eating behaviour and attitudes toward food, weight or body image. They are serious health conditions and are not simply lifestyle choices.

Key idea: compassion, early recognition and appropriate professional support matter.
06

ADHD & Neurodevelopmental Disorders

Neurodevelopmental conditions begin during development and can affect attention, learning, communication, behaviour, sensory processing or social interaction. ADHD is one example.

Key idea: different ways of thinking and processing information are not the same as being less capable.
07

Psychotic Disorders

Psychotic disorders can affect a person's perception, thinking and interpretation of reality. Experiences may include unusual beliefs, perceptions or disorganized thinking.

Key idea: early professional assessment can be important when significant changes in perception or thinking occur.
08

Disruptive & Dissocial Disorders

These involve persistent patterns of behaviour that can significantly interfere with relationships, learning or everyday functioning and go beyond ordinary developmental challenges.

Key idea: understanding context, development and environment is essential.
CONNECT

Mental and physical health are connected.

Mental and physical health influence one another across the life course. Many determinants and risk factors are shared.

01

Physical activity

Regular movement supports physical health and can also contribute to wellbeing, sleep and stress management.

02

Sleep

Sleep is closely connected with mood, attention, learning, energy and overall health.

03

Substance use

Tobacco, alcohol and other substances can affect both physical and mental health. Prevention and early support are important.

04

Social conditions

Housing, education, income, discrimination, safety, belonging and access to care can shape both mental and physical health.

REFLECT

Stigma can become another barrier to health.

Stigma occurs when people are judged, stereotyped or treated unfairly because of a mental health condition or because they seek support.

Stigma can discourage people from talking about their experiences or seeking appropriate help. We can reduce it through accurate information, respectful language, inclusion and listening without judgment.

Instead of: “What's wrong with you?”
Try: “How are you doing? Do you want to talk?”
Remember: A person is not their diagnosis.
EQUITY • SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Who has the opportunity to be mentally healthy?

Mental health is shaped not only by individual factors but also by the conditions in which people are born, grow, learn, work, live and age.

SELF

My wellbeing

Sleep, activity, relationships, coping skills and access to trustworthy information.

FAMILY & PEERS

My relationships

Belonging, safety, communication, support and acceptance.

COMMUNITY

My environment

Schools, neighbourhoods, recreation, culture and community services.

COUNTRY

My society

Education, employment, housing, social protection, healthcare and policies.

WORLD

Our global environment

Conflict, displacement, climate change, inequality, global crises and international cooperation.

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

10 Mental Health Disorders MCQs

Choose the best answer. These questions test understanding, not diagnosis.

1. Which statement best describes a mental health disorder?
Answer: B. A condition involving persistent or significant difficulties that can affect wellbeing or everyday functioning.
2. Which statement about anxiety is most accurate?
Answer: C. Anxiety is a normal human response, but anxiety disorders involve excessive or persistent difficulties that can interfere with life.
3. Depression is best distinguished from an ordinary bad day by which feature?
Answer: A. Persistent symptoms that can substantially affect mood, interest, energy or everyday functioning.
4. Which statement about bipolar disorder is correct?
Answer: D. It involves episodes of significant changes in mood, energy and activity, including elevated or activated and depressive episodes.
5. What is an important characteristic of PTSD?
Answer: B. It can involve persistent distress and other symptoms following exposure to traumatic or extremely threatening experiences.
6. Which statement about eating disorders is most appropriate?
Answer: C. Eating disorders are serious health conditions involving disturbances in eating behaviour and related thoughts or attitudes.
7. ADHD belongs to which broad category?
Answer: A. Neurodevelopmental disorders.
8. Why is stigma harmful?
Answer: D. Stigma can lead to stereotyping, discrimination, isolation and reluctance to seek appropriate support.
9. Which is an example of a social determinant that can influence mental health?
Answer: B. Conditions such as housing, education, income, safety, discrimination and access to healthcare.
10. What is the most appropriate response if you are worried about your own or someone else's mental health?
Answer: C. Talk with a trusted person and, when appropriate, seek support from a qualified health or mental health professional.
