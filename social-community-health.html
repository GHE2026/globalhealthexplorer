Social & Community Health

Health is shaped not only by what happens inside our bodies, but also by our relationships, our sense of belonging, our communities and the conditions that allow people to participate, feel safe and thrive.

01 · WHY THIS MATTERS HEALTH IS SOCIAL

MORE THAN INDIVIDUAL CHOICE

Our health is connected to the people and places around us.

A healthy life is easier when people have supportive relationships, opportunities to participate, safe spaces, inclusive schools and communities, and access to the resources they need.

Social & Community Health asks a bigger question: what conditions help people connect, belong and participate?

01

Connection

Relationships can provide companionship, practical help, encouragement and a sense of belonging.

02

Participation

Communities become healthier when people can participate in decisions, activities and shared spaces.

03

Equity

Not everyone has the same opportunities for connection, safety or participation. Health equity asks why.

02 · PERSONAL HINTS START WITH EVERYDAY LIFE

Small actions can strengthen connection.

Social health does not mean being surrounded by people all the time. It means having opportunities for relationships and connection that are meaningful, safe and appropriate for you.

Belonging

Notice places where you feel welcomed and respected.

Listen

Give people your attention and make space for different experiences.

Reach out

A simple conversation can be a meaningful act of connection.

Ask for help

Seeking support is a health skill, not a weakness.

Include others

Look for ways to make activities and spaces welcoming.

Contribute

Helping with a shared activity can strengthen community.

03 · GLOBAL HEALTH AT A GLANCE WHO · 2025
1 in 6 people worldwide are estimated to experience loneliness.
21% of young people in the WHO evidence base reported loneliness.
24% of people in low-income countries reported loneliness, compared with about 11% in high-income countries.
871,000 deaths per year are estimated by WHO to be associated with loneliness.

These figures do not mean that every person who feels lonely is unhealthy. They show why social connection deserves attention as a public-health issue.

04 · EXPLORE KEY CONCEPTS

What do we mean by social and community health?

Social connection

The ways people relate to and interact with one another. WHO describes three dimensions: the structure of our relationships, the support they provide, and their quality.

Social isolation

An objective state in which a person has too few relationships, roles or interactions with others.

Loneliness

A subjective feeling that occurs when the connections a person has do not match the connections they want or need.

Community health

The health and wellbeing of people living, learning, working and participating within shared communities and systems.

Important distinction:

A person can be alone without being lonely, and a person can feel lonely while surrounded by others.

05 · LEARN RELATIONSHIPS → COMMUNITY

The building blocks of social health

01

Healthy relationships

Healthy relationships involve respect, communication, trust, boundaries and the ability to disagree without harming one another.

02

Belonging

Belonging means feeling that you are accepted, valued and able to participate without having to hide important parts of yourself.

03

Peers & social norms

People influence one another. Understanding social norms can help us distinguish healthy influence from pressure or exclusion.

04

Family & generations

Families and intergenerational relationships can provide knowledge, care, identity and practical support.

05

Participation

Joining school, community, cultural, recreational or service activities can create opportunities for connection and contribution.

06

Safe & inclusive spaces

Parks, libraries, schools, recreation spaces, community centres and other shared places can support social connection when they are accessible and welcoming.

06 · THINK LOOK BENEATH THE SURFACE

ASK WHY

Is connection only a personal responsibility?

Who has access to welcoming spaces?

Think about transportation, cost, disability access, safety and geography.

Who gets heard?

Participation is not meaningful if decisions are made without listening to the people affected.

What creates exclusion?

Discrimination, stigma, poverty, displacement and inaccessible environments can limit connection.

What can communities change?

Sometimes the best solution is not asking an individual to adapt, but changing the environment around them.

07 · HEALTHY CHOICES RELATIONSHIPS & RESPECT

Building healthier relationships

Respect

Treat people as people, even when their experiences, identities or opinions differ from yours.

Boundaries

Healthy relationships include the ability to say no, communicate limits and respect other people's boundaries.

Communication

Listen carefully, ask questions and avoid assuming that you know what another person is experiencing.

Conflict resolution

Disagreement is normal. Safe problem-solving focuses on understanding, communication and appropriate support.

Inclusion

Notice who is being left out and consider what could make participation easier.

Support

If a situation is serious or unsafe, involve a trusted adult or qualified professional rather than trying to manage it alone.

08 · SDH LENS CONDITIONS SHAPE OPPORTUNITY

Social connection is influenced by the conditions around us.

The social determinants of health include the conditions in which people are born, grow, live, work and age, as well as access to power, money and resources.

Income

Can people afford to participate in activities and access transportation?

Education

Do people have opportunities to learn, participate and develop skills?

Housing

Are people living in safe, stable and appropriate homes?

Accessibility

Can people with different abilities participate fully?

Discrimination

Are some groups facing barriers because of stigma or unequal treatment?

Neighbourhoods

Are there safe, accessible spaces for people to meet, move, learn and participate?

10 · COMMUNITY HEALTH IN ACTION PARTICIPATE • LISTEN • CO-CREATE

Healthy communities are built with people, not just for people.

Community engagement can help ensure that policies and programmes reflect people's lived experiences and priorities. WHO identifies community and civil-society participation as an important part of advancing health equity.

01

Schools

Create inclusive opportunities for learning, participation, peer connection and student voice.

02

Neighbourhoods

Support safe and accessible public spaces where people can meet and participate.

03

Youth organizations

Give young people meaningful opportunities to contribute, lead and collaborate.

04

Recreation

Accessible recreation can create opportunities for movement, connection and belonging.

05

Volunteering

Service can connect people with shared goals and strengthen community participation.

06

Community-led solutions

People who experience a problem often have important knowledge about what solutions are realistic and useful.

13 · GLOBAL HEALTH & EVIDENCE FOLLOW THE EVIDENCE

WHAT THE EVIDENCE TELLS US

Social connection is a public-health issue.

In 2025, the WHO Commission on Social Connection released its landmark report calling for social connection to be recognised and resourced as a global public-health priority.

The World Health Assembly also adopted its first resolution on social connection in May 2025, recognising social connection as an important determinant of health.

The evidence points toward action at multiple levels: individual, community, institutional and policy.

Key evidence sources

  • WHO Commission on Social Connection
  • WHO — Social Connection
  • WHO — Social Isolation and Loneliness
  • WHO — Social Determinants of Health
  • WHO — World Report on Social Determinants of Health Equity

15 · TEST YOURSELF 12 MCQs

Global Health Explorer Quiz

Choose an answer, then open the explanation to check your thinking.

1. What is social connection?

Correct answer: B

Social connection describes how people relate to and interact with one another.

2. Which statement best describes loneliness?

Correct answer: C

Loneliness is a subjective feeling that occurs when desired or needed connection differs from actual connection.

3. Which statement about social isolation is most accurate?

Correct answer: A

Social isolation refers to an objective lack of sufficient relationships, roles or interactions.

4. According to WHO, approximately how many people globally experience loneliness?

Correct answer: B

WHO estimates that approximately one in six people globally experience loneliness.

5. Which is an example of a social determinant that can affect community participation?

Correct answer: D

Income, transportation, housing and accessibility can all influence opportunities for participation.

6. Which approach best reflects a healthy relationship?

Correct answer: A

Respect, communication and appropriate boundaries are important features of healthy relationships.

7. Why is inclusion important for community health?

Correct answer: C

Exclusion can create unequal opportunities to participate and benefit from community resources.

8. Which question best reflects a health-equity approach?

Correct answer: D

Asking who faces barriers and why helps identify structural and social causes of unequal participation.

9. What does meaningful community participation involve?

Correct answer: B

People affected by an issue should have meaningful opportunities to contribute to decisions and solutions.

10. A youth group discovers that its meetings are difficult for some students to attend. What is the best first Global Health Explorer question?

Correct answer: C

Ask which barriers are preventing participation and involve affected students in identifying solutions.

11. Which SDG is most directly connected to reducing inequalities in participation?

Correct answer: A

SDG 10 focuses on reducing inequalities within and among countries.

12. Which action best demonstrates global-health leadership?

Correct answer: D

Listening to affected people, examining evidence, identifying inequities and co-creating a realistic solution demonstrates responsible leadership.
