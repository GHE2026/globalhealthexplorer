Infectious Diseases, Vaccination & Prevention

Explore how infections spread, how they can be prevented, and why healthy communities depend on people, health systems and global cooperation.

GHE PRINCIPLE I can make healthy choices for myself, and I can help create healthier conditions for others.
WHY IT MATTERS

Infectious diseases are both biological and social challenges

Infectious diseases can affect individuals, families, communities and entire countries. Their prevention and control depend not only on individual behaviour, but also on vaccination systems, safe water, sanitation, food safety, housing, ventilation, healthcare access, surveillance and effective public-health systems.

Think beyond the individual.

A person can make healthy choices, but the conditions surrounding that person also shape exposure, vulnerability, prevention and access to care.

Ten Things Every Global Health Explorer Should Know

1

Understanding Infectious Diseases

Infectious diseases can be caused by bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites. Infestations such as scabies and head lice involve organisms living on or in the human body.

Think: Why do different infectious agents require different prevention and treatment approaches?
2

How Infections Spread — Especially Through the Air

Respiratory infections can spread through particles released when people breathe, speak, cough or sneeze. Crowding, poor ventilation and prolonged indoor exposure can increase transmission risk for some infections.

GHE Airborne Health Lens

Ventilation • Indoor air quality • Crowding • Respiratory hygiene • Staying home when ill

3

Vaccination & Community Protection

Vaccines prepare the immune system to respond to specific infections. Vaccination programmes also depend on health-system planning, supply chains, trained professionals, monitoring and equitable access.

Think: Why is vaccination both an individual and population-health intervention?
4

Antimicrobial Resistance & One Health

Antimicrobial resistance occurs when microorganisms develop the ability to survive medicines that once worked against them.

HUMANS ↔ ANIMALS ↔ FOOD SYSTEMS ↔ ENVIRONMENT

This interconnected perspective is known as One Health.

5

Foodborne & Waterborne Diseases

Contaminated food and water can transmit bacterial, viral and parasitic infections.

  • Campylobacter
  • Salmonella
  • Shiga toxin-producing E. coli
  • Listeria monocytogenes
  • Brucella
  • Hepatitis A and E
Food Safety Reminder

Unpasteurized milk and dairy products can carry dangerous pathogens even when the food appears fresh or natural.

6

TB, HIV & Malaria

Tuberculosis, HIV and malaria remain major global infectious-disease challenges.

  • TB: primarily an airborne bacterial infection
  • HIV: a viral infection that can be effectively managed with antiretroviral treatment
  • Malaria: a parasitic disease transmitted mainly through infected mosquitoes
GLOBAL HEALTH SPOTLIGHT

The Global Fund supports countries in the fight against HIV, tuberculosis and malaria while also strengthening health systems and community responses.

Explore Global Fund evidence →
7

Hepatitis & Major Viral Infections

Hepatitis refers to inflammation of the liver. Different hepatitis viruses have different routes of transmission, prevention strategies and treatments.

Hepatitis A Hepatitis B Hepatitis C Hepatitis D Hepatitis E

This is an important reminder that there is no single prevention strategy for all infectious diseases.

8

Parasites, Infestations & Neglected Tropical Diseases

Some infectious diseases receive relatively little attention despite affecting large numbers of people, particularly populations experiencing poverty and limited access to safe water, sanitation and healthcare.

NEGLECTED TROPICAL DISEASES

Examples include schistosomiasis, lymphatic filariasis, onchocerciasis, leishmaniasis, trachoma, soil-transmitted helminth infections and Chagas disease.

Neglect is often connected to inequality, visibility and global priority-setting.

9

Vector-Borne & Zoonotic Diseases

Vectors such as mosquitoes and ticks can transmit infectious agents. Zoonotic diseases can move between animals and humans.

🌱 Environment 🐾 Animals 🦟 Vectors 👥 Humans

Environmental and climate changes can alter some infectious-disease risks and distributions.

10

Outbreaks, Pandemics & the Health System

Infectious-disease control requires functioning health systems at multiple levels.

LOCAL REGIONAL NATIONAL GLOBAL

Depending on the jurisdiction, responsible authorities may monitor disease activity, conduct surveillance, investigate cases and clusters, provide prevention services, coordinate laboratory systems, notify and report diseases, communicate risks and coordinate outbreak responses.

Important: The exact responsibilities differ between countries and jurisdictions, but infectious-disease control cannot depend on individuals alone.
