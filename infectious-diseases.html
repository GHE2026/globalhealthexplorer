Infectious & Communicable Diseases

From microbes to communities. From prevention to preparedness.

Infectious diseases are among humanity's oldest health challenges — yet they remain central to the future of global health. Explore how infections emerge, spread and affect people differently, and how vaccination, evidence, innovation, health systems, One Health and global cooperation can protect communities.

GHE PRINCIPLE Learn how infections spread. Think critically about prevention. Connect health with society and the environment. Act responsibly.

WHY IT MATTERS

Infectious diseases are biological, social and global challenges

An infection may begin with a microorganism, but its consequences depend on much more: housing, nutrition, education, water and sanitation, climate, mobility, behaviour, healthcare access, surveillance, technology and the strength of health systems.

↔
Think beyond the pathogen.

Understanding infectious disease means understanding both biology and the conditions that shape exposure, vulnerability, prevention, treatment and recovery.

START WITH THE BASICS

Infectious does not always mean communicable

Infectious diseases are caused by pathogenic organisms. Communicable diseases are infectious diseases that can be transmitted between hosts. Understanding this distinction helps us think accurately about transmission and prevention.

🦠 BROADER CATEGORY

Infectious Diseases

Diseases caused by pathogenic microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites.

Key idea: An infectious disease does not necessarily have to be transmitted from one person to another.
→ SOME ARE
🌍 TRANSMISSIBLE SUBSET

Communicable Diseases

Infectious diseases that can be transmitted directly or indirectly between hosts, including some infections that move between animals and humans.

Key idea: Transmission requires a route by which an infectious agent can reach another susceptible host.
✓
THE GHE GOLDEN RULE

All communicable diseases are infectious — but not all infectious diseases are communicable.

!
EXAMPLE

Tetanus: infectious, but not normally communicable

Tetanus is caused by Clostridium tetani. The organism can enter the body through contaminated wounds. Tetanus is therefore infectious, but it is not normally transmitted from person to person.

THINK SYSTEMICALLY

The Chain of Infection

Transmission is not inevitable. Infectious-disease prevention often works by interrupting one or more links in the chain between an infectious agent and a susceptible host.

01

Infectious Agent

The microorganism capable of causing infection.

Virus • Bacterium • Fungus • Parasite
02

Reservoir

The environment or host in which an infectious agent normally lives and may multiply.

People • Animals • Water • Environment
03

Portal of Exit

The route by which an infectious agent leaves its reservoir.

Respiratory secretions • Blood • Stool
04

Mode of Transmission

The mechanism by which an infectious agent reaches another host.

Air • Food • Water • Vectors • Contact
05

Portal of Entry

The route through which an infectious agent enters a new host.

Respiratory tract • Skin • Digestive tract
06

Susceptible Host

A person or animal who may become infected depending on immunity, exposure and other factors.

Immunity • Age • Health • Exposure
Prevention can interrupt the chain. Vaccination, clean water, food safety, ventilation, vector control, infection prevention, appropriate treatment and surveillance are examples of different ways societies can reduce transmission.
EXPLORE TRANSMISSION

How Can Infections Spread?

Different pathogens have different transmission pathways. One disease may also have more than one route of transmission.

01

Respiratory

Some infections spread through respiratory particles or aerosols, especially during close or prolonged exposure.

Influenza • COVID-19 • Measles • TB
02

Food & Water

Contaminated food and water can transmit many bacterial, viral and parasitic infections.

Salmonella • Cholera • Hepatitis A
03

Vector-Borne

Vectors such as mosquitoes and ticks can transmit infectious agents between hosts.

Malaria • Dengue • Lyme disease
04

Blood & Body Fluids

Some infections can be transmitted through particular exposures to infected blood or body fluids.

HIV • Hepatitis B • Hepatitis C
05

Zoonotic

Some infections can move between animals and humans.

Rabies • Brucellosis • Some influenza viruses
06

Sexually Transmitted

Some infections are transmitted through sexual contact, with prevention depending on the specific infection.

HPV • HIV • Syphilis
07

Environmental & Soil-Associated

Some pathogens persist in soil, water or other environments and can reach people through specific exposures.

Tetanus • Soil-transmitted helminths
08

Multiple Routes

Some diseases have more than one important transmission pathway or transmission context.

Transmission depends on the pathogen
KNOW THE GLOBAL LANDSCAPE

Diseases Every Global Health Explorer Should Know

No single list can represent every infectious disease. These learning clusters provide a practical gateway into some of the diseases, disease groups and public-health challenges that matter globally.

01

Tuberculosis — TB

Tuberculosis is caused by bacteria of the Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex. TB most commonly affects the lungs and can spread through the air when people with infectious pulmonary TB release infectious particles.

THINK GLOBAL HEALTH

TB connects microbiology with poverty, housing, ventilation, diagnosis, treatment, drug resistance, health systems and equity.

02

HIV & AIDS

HIV is a virus that attacks the immune system. Effective antiretroviral therapy can suppress the virus and allow people living with HIV to live healthy lives.

03

Malaria

Malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasites and is transmitted mainly through infected mosquitoes. It remains a major global health challenge, particularly in tropical and subtropical regions.

COVID-19 & Respiratory Epidemics

COVID-19 demonstrated how a respiratory pathogen can rapidly affect individuals, communities, economies and health systems. It also highlighted the importance of surveillance, scientific evidence, communication, vaccination and preparedness.

Influenza & Emerging Respiratory Threats

Influenza viruses can cause seasonal epidemics and, when novel viruses acquire the ability to spread efficiently between people, can create pandemic threats.

06

Viral Hepatitis A–E

Viral hepatitis is not one disease with one transmission route. Hepatitis A, B, C, D and E differ in how they spread, how they are prevented and how they are treated.

07

HPV & Cervical Cancer Prevention

Human papillomavirus (HPV) is a very common infection. Certain HPV types can cause cancers, including cervical cancer. HPV vaccination is an important prevention strategy.

PREVENTION

Vaccination + screening + appropriate follow-up can substantially reduce the burden of cervical cancer.

Neglected Tropical Diseases

WHO recognizes a diverse group of neglected tropical diseases affecting populations that often experience poverty, limited access to healthcare, unsafe water and sanitation, and other structural disadvantages.

FOOD • WATER • SANITATION

Food- & Water-Borne Diseases

Safe food and water are fundamental public-health protections. Foodborne illness can result from bacteria, viruses, parasites and other hazards.

01

Salmonella

Salmonella can cause gastrointestinal illness and can be transmitted through contaminated food and other exposure pathways.

02

Campylobacter

Campylobacter is an important cause of bacterial gastroenteritis and is associated with contaminated food and animal sources.

03

Shiga Toxin-Producing E. coli

Certain strains of E. coli can cause serious foodborne illness. Food safety and prevention of contamination are critical.

04

Listeria

Listeria monocytogenes can cause serious illness, particularly in certain higher-risk populations.

05

Cholera

Cholera is strongly connected to unsafe water, sanitation and hygiene conditions.

06

Typhoid

Typhoid fever is caused by Salmonella Typhi and is associated with contaminated food and water.

07

Hepatitis A & E

Hepatitis A and E can be associated with fecal-oral transmission and contaminated food or water.

08

Food Safety

Safe preparation, cooking, storage, hygiene and prevention of contamination are important layers of protection.

A clean-looking food may still be contaminated. Food safety depends on systems as well as individual practices: farms, processing, transportation, markets, restaurants, households, inspection and public-health surveillance all matter.
ONE HEALTH

Diseases Shared Across Humans, Animals & Environments

Zoonotic diseases demonstrate why human health cannot always be separated from animal health and environmental change.

01

Rabies

A viral disease that can be transmitted from infected animals to people. Prevention depends on animal vaccination, exposure prevention and timely public-health measures.

02

Brucellosis

A bacterial zoonosis associated with infected animals and animal products. It illustrates the connection between veterinary and human health.

03

Avian & Animal Influenza

Influenza viruses circulating in animals can create opportunities for human infection and require coordinated surveillance.

04

Lyme Disease & Other Tick-Borne Infections

Tick-borne diseases demonstrate how vectors, wildlife, landscapes, climate and human behaviour interact.

VECTORS • ENVIRONMENT • CLIMATE

Vector-Borne Diseases

Mosquitoes, ticks and other vectors can transmit infectious agents. Vector-borne disease risk is influenced by ecology, climate, land use, housing and human movement.

01

Malaria

Parasites transmitted mainly by infected mosquitoes.

02

Dengue

A viral infection transmitted mainly by infected mosquitoes.

03

West Nile Virus

A mosquito-borne viral infection associated with birds and mosquito transmission cycles.

04

Leishmaniasis

A parasitic disease transmitted by infected sand flies.

BEYOND BACTERIA & VIRUSES

Fungal & Parasitic Diseases

Infectious disease is much broader than viruses and bacteria. Fungi and parasites cause major illness and can be especially important when immune systems are weakened or when environmental conditions favour transmission.

01

Fungal Diseases

Fungal infections range from common skin infections to serious invasive diseases. Examples include ringworm, candidiasis and some environmentally acquired fungal infections.

02

Parasitic Diseases

Parasites include organisms such as protozoa and helminths. Their transmission can involve vectors, food, water, soil or direct exposure.

03

Schistosomiasis

A parasitic disease associated with freshwater exposure in endemic settings.

04

Soil-Transmitted Helminths

Parasitic worms associated with contaminated soil and conditions involving sanitation and hygiene.

PREVENTION

Vaccine-Preventable Diseases

Vaccination is one of the most important tools for preventing infectious diseases. But vaccination programmes are also complex health-system interventions requiring evidence, supply chains, trained professionals, surveillance, communication and equitable access.

01

Measles

A highly contagious viral infection that can be prevented through vaccination.

02

Polio

A viral disease for which global eradication efforts continue.

03

Pertussis

A bacterial respiratory infection for which vaccination provides important protection.

04

HPV

Vaccination against HPV is an important cancer-prevention strategy.

05

Hepatitis B

Vaccination can prevent hepatitis B infection.

06

Tetanus

Vaccination protects against tetanus, which is not normally transmitted between people.

A GLOBAL THREAT

Antimicrobial Resistance — AMR

Antimicrobial resistance occurs when microorganisms change in ways that make medicines less effective against them. AMR can make infections harder to prevent and treat and threatens modern medicine.

↔
ONE HEALTH CONNECTION

AMR is influenced by antimicrobial use and transmission across human health, animal health, food systems and the environment. It therefore requires coordinated action across sectors.

PREPAREDNESS

Outbreaks, Epidemics & Pandemics

Infectious-disease events can range from localized outbreaks to epidemics affecting regions and pandemics affecting populations across countries and continents.

01

Detect

Surveillance and laboratory systems identify unusual disease patterns.

02

Investigate

Public-health teams investigate cases, clusters and possible sources of transmission.

03

Communicate

Clear, timely and evidence-informed risk communication helps communities make informed decisions.

04

Respond

Health systems coordinate prevention, clinical care, testing, vaccination and other appropriate interventions.

05

Learn

Every outbreak can generate evidence that improves future preparedness.

06

Prepare

Preparedness begins before the next outbreak occurs.

HEALTH SYSTEMS

Infectious Diseases Test Health Systems

A pathogen may be microscopic, but an effective response requires people, institutions, information, infrastructure and leadership.

01

Surveillance

Detecting and monitoring disease patterns.

02

Laboratories

Identifying pathogens and supporting diagnosis and surveillance.

03

Vaccines & Medicines

Ensuring appropriate access, supply and delivery.

04

Health Workforce

Healthcare and public-health professionals are essential to prevention and response.

05

Communication

Communities need trustworthy, understandable and timely information.

06

Global Cooperation

Infectious threats can cross borders, making cooperation essential.

SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Who Is Most at Risk — And Why?

Infectious diseases do not affect all populations equally. Exposure, vulnerability, access to prevention and access to treatment can all be shaped by social and economic conditions.

SDH

Social Determinants

Poverty, housing, crowding, education, employment, WASH, discrimination, nutrition, environment and healthcare access can influence infectious-disease risk.

SDG 3

Good Health

Prevent disease and promote health and well-being.

SDG 6

Clean Water

Water, sanitation and hygiene are fundamental to infectious disease prevention.

SDG 10

Reduced Inequalities

Health protection should not depend on where someone is born or how much they have.

SDG 13

Climate Action

Environmental change can alter some infectious-disease risks.
