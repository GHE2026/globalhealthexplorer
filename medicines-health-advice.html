START HERE

Medicines Are Powerful Tools

+

Benefit Requires Responsibility

A medicine may be helpful when it is the right medicine for the right person, used in the right way and monitored appropriately.

The goal is not to become your own doctor or pharmacist. The goal is to become a safe, informed participant in your health care.

What Can Go Wrong?

  • Using the wrong medicine
  • Using too much or too little
  • Missing important warnings
  • Combining medicines without checking
  • Following unreliable health advice
  • Using someone else's prescription medicine
  • Using antibiotics when they are not appropriate
  • Failing to tell a health professional about medicines being used
GHE SAFETY PRINCIPLE If you are unsure about a medicine, ask a qualified health professional rather than guessing.
02

WHY IS THIS IMPORTANT?

Medication Safety Is a Global Health Issue

Medicines are among the most important tools in modern health care. They can prevent disease, control conditions, treat infections and improve quality of life.

At the same time, unsafe medication practices can cause preventable harm. Medication safety is therefore not only an individual responsibility. It is also a responsibility of health professionals, pharmacies, hospitals, manufacturers, regulators and health systems.

WHO's Medication Without Harm initiative places medication safety within a broader patient-safety and health-system framework.

THINK GLOBAL

Safe medicines require safe systems.

Quality, availability, affordability, prescribing, dispensing, communication, monitoring and patient participation all matter.

EXPLORE

What Exactly Is a Medicine?

Medicines come in many forms and are used for many purposes. Understanding categories helps you ask better questions.

Rx

Prescription Medicines

Medicines that require authorization from an appropriate health professional under the rules of the country where they are prescribed.

OTC

Non-Prescription Medicines

Medicines that can be obtained without a prescription in appropriate circumstances, but still require careful attention to labels, warnings and instructions.

V

Vaccines

Vaccines help the immune system develop protection against specific infections and are an important population-health intervention.

AB

Antimicrobials

Medicines used against microorganisms. Different antimicrobials act against different types of organisms. Antibiotics specifically target bacteria.

05

LEARN

The Five Moments of Medication Safety

WHO's 5 Moments for Medication Safety provides a simple way to encourage patients and caregivers to participate actively in medication safety.

1

Starting a Medicine

What is this medicine for? What should I know before I start?

2

Taking My Medicine

Do I understand how I am supposed to use it?

3

Adding a Medicine

Have I told my health professional about other medicines or health products I use?

4

Reviewing My Medicines

Are all my medicines still appropriate and understood?

5

Stopping a Medicine

Should I stop, change or continue this medicine? Ask the appropriate health professional rather than making an important change based on a guess.

Remember: A medicine should not be started, stopped or changed simply because someone online says it is safe or effective for you.

HEALTH LITERACY IN ACTION

Read the Label — Don't Guess

Health Canada requires non-prescription medicines sold in Canada to provide important information in a standardized Drug Facts format.

Learning to identify the main parts of a medicine label is a practical health-literacy skill.

Active Ingredient What medicinal ingredient is present?
Purpose What does the ingredient do?
Uses What is the product intended to help with?
Warnings When might the product be inappropriate or require professional advice?
Directions What does the official label say about use?
Other Information What storage or other important information is provided?
!

The Golden Rule

If you do not understand a medicine label, ask a pharmacist or another qualified health professional. Do not rely on guesswork.

GLOBAL HEALTH

Antibiotics: Powerful — But Not for Every Infection

COMMON MISUNDERSTANDING

“Antibiotics treat every infection.”

Not true. Antibiotics are medicines used against bacteria. They do not treat viral infections simply because the illness makes someone feel unwell.

Antibiotic stewardship

Using antibiotics appropriately helps protect their effectiveness and reduce antimicrobial resistance.

Never share prescription medicines

A medicine prescribed for one person may not be appropriate for another person.

Never rely on leftover antibiotics

Decisions about antibiotics should be made by an appropriate health professional.

RESIST

Antimicrobial resistance is a global health challenge.

DIGITAL HEALTH

Health Advice Online: STOP → CHECK → COMPARE → ACT

01

STOP

Don't immediately believe, share or act on a health claim.

02

CHECK

Who created the information? Is the source identifiable, credible and current?

03

COMPARE

Does the claim agree with reliable public-health or professional sources?

04

ACT

For personal health decisions, discuss important questions with a qualified health professional.

Be especially careful with claims that:

Promise a “miracle cure” Say doctors are hiding the truth Depend only on testimonials Sell a product while claiming to be neutral Discourage professional advice Claim that one solution works for everyone

SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Safe Medicine Use Is Not Only About Individual Choice

Education

People need understandable information to make informed health decisions.

Income

Affordability can influence access to medicines and continuity of care.

Geography

Rural and remote communities may face different access challenges.

Language

Health information should be understandable and culturally appropriate.

Digital Access

Digital health information can expand access while also creating new misinformation and inequity challenges.

Health Systems

Safe prescribing, dispensing, monitoring and communication depend on functioning health systems.

GHE YOUNG INVESTIGATORS

Community Medicine Information Project

How easy is it for students or community members to find, understand and evaluate trustworthy information about medicines?

SUPERVISED LEARNING PROJECT
01 QUESTION

Define one simple health-literacy question.

02 PLAN

Agree on what information will and will not be collected.

03 SAFEGUARD

Work with a teacher or qualified health professional.

04 PILOT

Test the learning activity with a very small group.

05 COLLECT

Gather anonymous, voluntary responses.

06 ANALYZE

Look for patterns rather than identifying individuals.

07 COMMUNICATE

Present only aggregated findings.

08 ACT

Design a small educational improvement.

Protect People First

  • Participation should be voluntary.
  • Follow school, teacher and local requirements for consent/assent and permissions.
  • Do not collect names, addresses, diagnoses, medication lists or other sensitive personal information.
  • Keep responses anonymous whenever possible.
  • Report results only in aggregate.
  • If the project becomes formal human-subject research, follow the applicable research-ethics/REB process.

MEASURE WHAT MATTERS

Health Literacy Measurement

Researchers and health systems use validated instruments to study different dimensions of health literacy. These instruments should not be confused with informal quizzes or classroom activities.

HLQ

Health Literacy Questionnaire

The Health Literacy Questionnaire (HLQ) is a validated multidimensional instrument developed to measure a broad range of health-literacy needs and experiences.

Validated instrument Multidimensional

GHE does not reproduce the questionnaire items here. Researchers should consult the original publication and appropriate permissions or licensing arrangements.

NVS

Newest Vital Sign

The Newest Vital Sign is a brief health-literacy screening instrument that uses a nutrition-label task to assess aspects of reading comprehension and numeracy.

Brief screening tool Widely studied

Use in educational or research settings should follow the instrument's validated protocol and applicable permissions.

HLS19

WHO Europe Population Health Literacy Survey

WHO Europe's HLS19/M-POHL work provides a population-level framework for measuring health literacy across countries, including dimensions related to digital health literacy.

Population research International
Important distinction

A classroom quiz created by GHE is an educational exercise. It is not automatically a validated health-literacy instrument.
