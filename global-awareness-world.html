Our Interconnected World

Discover how people, environments, cultures, movement, technology and health are connected across our shared world.

A political border can organize countries, but many forces that influence health move through air, water, ecosystems, travel and human networks.

GLOBAL AWARENESS PATHWAY
01 · EXPLORE

How are we connected?

Global awareness begins with seeing the links among people, places, environments, economies, culture and health.

THE BIG IDEA

We share connected systems.

People move. Goods move. Information moves. Air and water move. Animals, ecosystems and infectious agents can move too. Our world is interconnected even when political borders are not.

A QUESTION TO EXPLORE

Can a border stop nature?

A political boundary can organize governments, but it cannot stop an ocean current, wind-borne dust, wildfire smoke or every infectious disease from moving across regions.

02 · LEARN

Nature does not always recognize borders.

Different hazards move through different pathways. Understanding those pathways helps us understand why cooperation matters.

Viruses & pathogens Can travel with people, animals, food or water.
Dust Wind can carry dust across very large distances.
PM₂.₅ Fine particles from pollution can travel across regions.
O₃ — ground-level ozone Can form from pollutants and affect air quality over large areas.
UV radiation Solar radiation reaches every country; exposure depends on place, time and behaviour.
Wildfire smoke Smoke can cross provinces, states and countries.
Oil spills Ocean currents, wind and waves can spread pollution.
Nuclear & radiological hazards Some incidents can create exposure pathways through air, water or other systems.
The pathways are different—but the lesson is shared: health risks can become transboundary risks.
03 · THINK

Connections shape health.

Global awareness also means understanding differences in opportunity and the reasons people move.

Culture & diversity Different histories, languages, identities and ways of knowing enrich our shared world.
Migration & movement People move for study, work, family, safety, climate and many other reasons.
Inequality & equity People and communities do not begin with the same opportunities or resources.
Climate & environment A healthy planet supports human health; environmental risks can cross borders.
GLOBAL CONNECTION

From local lives to global goals.

Global citizenship means understanding connections and taking responsibility for how our choices affect others.

SDGs

A shared plan for a healthier, fairer world.

The Sustainable Development Goals connect health with education, equality, climate, water, cities, peace, partnerships and more.

GLOBAL CITIZENSHIP

Think beyond the border.

Listen to different perspectives, respect diversity, question unfairness, use evidence and recognize that local action can contribute to global change.
