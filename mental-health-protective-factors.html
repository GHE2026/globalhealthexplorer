EXPLORE

What are protective factors?

Protective factors are characteristics, relationships, resources or conditions that can support wellbeing and reduce vulnerability to mental health difficulties.

01

They interact

Protective factors rarely work alone. Their effects can reinforce one another.

02

They can change

Some protective factors can be strengthened through relationships, education, policy and supportive environments.

03

They are not guarantees

Having protective factors does not mean a person can never experience mental health difficulties.

LEARN

Eight protective factors worth understanding

1

Healthy Sleep

Regular sleep supports learning, emotional regulation, physical health and everyday functioning.

2

Physical Activity

Regular movement can support physical health, mood, stress regulation and social participation.

3

Social Connection

Supportive relationships can provide belonging, encouragement and practical help.

4

Healthy Coping

Problem-solving, emotional awareness, relaxation and constructive coping skills can support wellbeing.

5

Meaning & Purpose

Learning, creativity, contribution, goals and meaningful activities can support a sense of purpose.

6

Belonging

Feeling accepted and included at home, school and in the community can support wellbeing.

7

Safe Environments

Safety, stability and freedom from violence and discrimination are important foundations for health.

8

Access to Support

Accessible schools, communities and health services can help people receive support when they need it.

RESILIENCE

Resilience is built through relationships and resources.

Resilience does not mean being strong all the time or handling every problem alone.

It can develop through supportive relationships, useful skills, opportunities to learn, access to resources and environments that allow people to recover and grow.

Connect Build supportive relationships.
Learn Develop useful coping skills.
Participate Find meaningful activities.
Seek Use support when needed.

EQUITY & SOCIAL DETERMINANTS

Protective factors are not equally available to everyone.

It is not enough to tell individuals to “be resilient.” Public health must also ask whether people have equitable access to the conditions that support resilience.

  • Can families access safe housing?
  • Are schools supportive and inclusive?
  • Are recreational opportunities affordable?
  • Can people access mental-health services?
  • Are discrimination and exclusion being addressed?

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

10 Questions — Protective Factors

1. What is a protective factor?

Correct answer: B. A characteristic, relationship, resource or condition that can support wellbeing or reduce vulnerability.

2. Which is an example of social protection?

Correct answer: C. Supportive relationships that provide belonging and help.

3. Does one protective factor guarantee good mental health?

Correct answer: A. No. Mental health results from interacting factors.

4. Why is sleep important?

Correct answer: D. Sleep supports learning, emotional regulation and physical health.

5. Which statement about resilience is best?

Correct answer: B. Resilience can be supported by relationships, skills and resources.

6. Why does belonging matter?

Correct answer: C. Belonging can support connection, identity and social support.

7. Why is an equity lens important?

Correct answer: A. Protective resources are not equally available to all people.

8. Which is a community-level protective factor?

Correct answer: D. Safe, inclusive schools and accessible community services.

9. Which SDG is directly connected to wellbeing?

Correct answer: B. SDG 3 — Good Health and Well-being.

10. Which statement best reflects public health?

Correct answer: A. Public health should strengthen both individual resources and the environments in which people live.
