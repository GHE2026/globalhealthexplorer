Personal Hygiene

Personal hygiene is more than keeping clean. It is a set of everyday practices that can help protect health, support dignity, prevent illness, strengthen confidence, and contribute to healthier families and communities.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Hygiene is personal — and also global.

What we do every day can influence our own health, the health of people around us, and the conditions in which communities live.

01

Protect Yourself

Good hygiene practices can reduce exposure to germs, protect the skin and mouth, and support everyday health and comfort.

02

Protect Others

Some infections can spread between people. Responsible hygiene can therefore be an act of care for family members, classmates, friends, and the wider community.

03

Support Dignity

Personal care is connected to comfort, confidence, participation, relationships, and the ability to take part in everyday life.

PERSONAL HINTS

Start with practical habits.

There is no need to make hygiene complicated. Focus on consistent, realistic habits that fit your everyday life.

01

Know your routine

Identify the personal-care habits that belong naturally in your morning, evening, school, sports, and other daily routines.

02

Be consistent

Healthy habits are generally more useful when they are practical enough to maintain over time.

03

Be respectful

People have different cultures, resources, routines, bodies, and circumstances. Avoid judging others based on appearance or personal-care practices.

04

Ask when unsure

Reliable health information can help you distinguish ordinary questions from concerns that deserve professional advice.

LEARN

Hygiene works through simple protective behaviours.

Hygiene is not about perfection. It is about understanding how everyday behaviours can affect health and making informed choices.

01

Hands can carry microorganisms.

Hands frequently come into contact with surfaces, objects, food, and other people. Appropriate hand hygiene can interrupt pathways through which infections spread.

02

Oral health is part of overall health.

Teeth and gums need regular care. Oral health can influence comfort, eating, communication, confidence, and quality of life.

03

Cleanliness has a context.

Good hygiene depends not only on individual choices but also on access to clean water, sanitation, hygiene facilities, education, products, and safe environments.

04

Health advice should be evidence-informed.

Not every health claim on social media is reliable. Learning how to evaluate health information is part of becoming health literate.

SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Hygiene is influenced by the conditions around us.

Health is shaped by more than individual behaviour. Social, economic, environmental, and structural conditions can make healthy choices easier or harder.

Water & Sanitation

Reliable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene facilities is fundamental to health and dignity.

Education

Health education can provide knowledge and practical skills, but education must be supported by accessible environments.

Economic Conditions

The cost and availability of hygiene products, dental care, clean water, and other resources can influence health opportunities.

Built Environment

Schools, homes, public buildings, transportation, and neighbourhood infrastructure can all affect hygiene and infection prevention.

Equity & Inclusion

Everyone should be able to maintain health and dignity regardless of income, ability, location, background, or circumstance.

Culture & Social Norms

Hygiene practices are shaped by culture, family traditions, community expectations, and social experiences.

GLOBAL LEADERSHIP & EVIDENCE

A health leader asks for evidence.

Global Health Explorers do not simply repeat health claims. They learn to ask where information comes from, what evidence supports it, and whether it applies to the situation they are considering.

ASK

What is the claim?

Clearly identify what someone is saying about hygiene or health.

CHECK

What is the evidence?

Look for trustworthy sources such as public health agencies, professional organizations, scientific evidence, and qualified health professionals.

THINK

Does it make sense here?

Consider age, context, culture, environment, access to resources, and the quality of available evidence.

SHARE

Communicate responsibly.

Share useful health information without exaggeration, stigma, fear, or unnecessary judgment.
