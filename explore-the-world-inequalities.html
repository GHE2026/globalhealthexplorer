GHE Data Detective

“Country A has twice the rate of Country B.”

Before deciding what this means, investigate the statement.

Your mission: Do not accept the headline until you have checked the indicator.
01

What rate?

Incidence, prevalence, mortality, coverage — or something else?

02

Which year?

Are both values from the same period?

03

Which population?

Same age range, sex, population definition and geographic level?

04

What is the denominator?

What population is the rate calculated from?

05

Who produced the estimate?

National authority, WHO, IHME or another source?

06

Observed or estimated?

What method produced the value? Is uncertainty reported?

07

Are the countries comparable?

Check definitions, methods, populations, time and context.

Important data-literacy lesson: some numbers are observed and some are estimated. Some datasets combine evidence from many sources. Always investigate the method, source and uncertainty before interpreting a result.
Knowledge Check

Three Data Detective Missions

Mission 1 — Which comparison is strongest?
Compare values using the same indicator definition, population, time period, geographic level and appropriate methodology. Same unit alone is not enough.
Mission 2 — A national average improves. Did every group improve?
Not necessarily. A national average can improve while important differences between population groups remain or even widen. Disaggregated data are needed to explore the pattern.
Mission 3 — A map shows a striking pattern. Does the map explain why?
No. A map can reveal a pattern. Explaining the pattern requires additional evidence, context and careful investigation.
