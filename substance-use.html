Substance Use, Smoking & Vaping

Understanding tobacco, nicotine, alcohol and other substances, and the conditions that influence health-related choices.

I can make healthy choices for myself, and I can help create healthier conditions for others.
OUR CORE MESSAGE

Health choices happen in real-world environments.

Young people's decisions are influenced by many factors: family, friends, school, stress, culture, marketing, availability, laws, income, community conditions and access to support.

GHE therefore does not teach substance use as simply a matter of “good” or “bad” people or “good” or “bad” choices. We explore individual responsibility together with the social and commercial conditions that can influence health.

01

Tobacco & Smoking

Tobacco use is one of the world's major preventable causes of disease and premature death. WHO states that all forms of tobacco use are harmful and that there is no safe level of exposure to tobacco smoke.

Why adolescence matters

Many people who use tobacco begin during adolescence. Preventing nicotine dependence early can protect health across the life course.

Explore WHO: Tobacco & Nicotine ↗
02

Nicotine & Vaping

Nicotine is highly addictive. Adolescents and young adults are particularly vulnerable because brain development continues into young adulthood.

Nicotine

A highly addictive substance that can affect the developing brain and contribute to dependence.

Vaping

E-cigarettes can expose users to nicotine and other potentially harmful substances.

Marketing

Product design, flavours, social media and marketing can influence how young people perceive nicotine products.

Explore current WHO evidence on tobacco and nicotine ↗
03

Alcohol

Alcohol contains ethanol, a psychoactive substance with dependence-producing properties. Alcohol use is associated with injuries, several cancers, cardiovascular disease, liver disease, mental-health conditions and other health consequences.

Look beyond the individual.

WHO's recent evidence emphasizes that young people's alcohol use can be influenced by the environments around them, including homes, schools and digital platforms, as well as availability, affordability and social acceptability.

Individual

Age, development, health and personal circumstances can influence vulnerability.

Social

Friends, family, culture and social expectations can shape attitudes and behaviour.

Commercial

Marketing, availability and affordability can influence the environment in which decisions are made.

Explore WHO: Alcohol ↗ Explore WHO 2025: Young People, Alcohol & Digital Environments ↗
04

Other Psychoactive Substances

Psychoactive substances can affect the brain and behaviour. Their effects and risks vary according to the substance, person, dose, context and pattern of use.

GHE safety principle

This page focuses on health literacy and prevention. GHE does not provide instructions for obtaining, preparing, concealing or using psychoactive substances.

Young people should be encouraged to seek appropriate support rather than attempting to diagnose or manage substance-related problems alone.

05

Why Adolescence Matters

Adolescence is a formative period of physical, cognitive and psychosocial development. Patterns of behaviour established during this period can influence health later in life.

Brain Development

Development continues throughout adolescence and young adulthood.

Identity

Young people develop independence and social identities.

Peer Influence

Relationships can strongly influence social norms and decisions.

Life-course Health

Early patterns can influence later health and wellbeing.

Health-related choices do not happen in a vacuum. Commercial practices can influence what products are available, how they are promoted and how people perceive them.

08

Mental Health, Stress & Substance Use

Mental health and substance use can intersect in complex ways. Substance use may sometimes be connected with attempts to cope with stress or emotional difficulties, while substance-related problems can also negatively affect mental wellbeing.

Think beyond “self-control”.

Understanding stress, relationships, trauma, social exclusion, poverty and access to support can help explain why prevention requires both individual and population-level approaches.

Explore WHO: Adolescent Mental Health ↗
09

Social Determinants of Health

Substance-use patterns can differ substantially between populations because the conditions in which people live, learn, work and grow are different.

Seeking help is a responsible choice.
