GLOBAL AWARENESS • THINK GLOBAL
WHY GLOBAL AWARENESS?

The world is connected.

A political border can organize countries and governments, but many forces that influence health do not stop at borders.

CONNECTION

What happens somewhere can affect somewhere else.

People, goods, information, infectious agents, pollution, smoke, dust, water and environmental hazards can move through interconnected systems.

RESPONSIBILITY

Global awareness leads to responsible participation.

Understanding our connections helps us recognize inequality, respect different perspectives, use evidence responsibly and contribute to healthier communities.

GHE INFORMATION CHECK

Pause before you believe—or share.

When health information is circulating, ask:

SOURCE — Who produced it?
DATE — Is it current?
EVIDENCE — What supports the claim?
CONTEXT — What might be missing?
CORROBORATION — Do reliable independent sources agree?
PURPOSE — Inform, persuade, entertain, sell or provoke?
SHARE? — Could sharing it cause harm?

For current public-health decisions, always verify with WHO and the appropriate government/public-health authority.

TRUSTED PUBLIC-HEALTH INFORMATION

Know where to look.

GHE teaches learners how to think critically about information—but GHE is not the authority that announces public-health emergencies or gives official public-health instructions.

PRIMARY AUTHORITIES

WHO & Government Public-Health Authorities

For current health threats, alerts, recommendations and official measures, go directly to the World Health Organization and the relevant government or public-health authority.

WHO Headquarters, Regional Offices and Country Offices may provide global and regional information, while governments and their public-health authorities make and implement decisions within their jurisdictions.

LEARNING EXTENSION

Learn more—but know the difference.

Educational resources can help learners understand epidemiology, global health and public-health concepts.

GHE may connect learners with educational resources such as NextGenU, but educational resources are not substitutes for current official public-health decisions.

THE GHE QUESTION

How does the world recognize and respond to a health threat?

And once a trusted public-health authority announces a public-health threat, what should I know and do?

UNDERSTAND

Recognize the system.

Surveillance, assessment, communication, coordination and response help societies understand changing health risks.

PARTICIPATE

Know your role.

Your role changes with the situation—but responsible participation always begins with reliable information, respect, appropriate action and care for others.
