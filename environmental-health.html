HEALTHY LIVING · SUBJECT 7

Environmental Health

Our health depends on the environments in which we live, learn, work and play. Explore how air, water, climate, chemicals, waste, energy, buildings and ecosystems shape health — and why healthier environments require action from individuals, communities and systems.

01

Why is Environmental Health Important?

PREVENTION

Environment is part of health

Health is shaped not only by healthcare and individual behaviour, but also by the air we breathe, the water we drink, the homes we live in and the environments around us.

WHO describes environmental health as involving conditions such as clean air, safe water and sanitation, climate, chemicals, radiation and healthy living and working environments.

EQUITY

Exposure is not equally distributed

People do not always have the same ability to avoid environmental hazards.

Housing, income, occupation, geography, infrastructure, age, disability and other social conditions can influence both exposure and the ability to respond.

SYSTEMS

Prevention requires systems

WHO, UNEP, CDC and Health Canada all emphasize that environmental health requires action beyond the individual.

Transport, energy, housing, water systems, industry, urban planning, environmental regulation and public health all matter.

GHE QUESTION

If everyone depends on the same environment, how should responsibility for protecting health be shared?

02

Personal Hints

Environmental health begins with curiosity: notice the conditions around you, ask questions, and learn how reliable evidence can guide action.

Notice your environment

  • What is the air like where you live and study?
  • Is your environment very hot, cold, noisy or crowded at certain times?
  • Where does your drinking water come from?
  • What environmental information is available in your community?

Protect yourself wisely

  • Pay attention to reliable air-quality and heat information when relevant.
  • Use shade, clothing and appropriate sun protection when UV exposure is high.
  • During extreme heat, stay hydrated and use cooler environments when available.
  • Follow trusted guidance about water, environmental hazards and emergencies.

Think beyond yourself

A healthy environment is not created simply by asking individuals to make better choices.

Ask whether people have the resources, infrastructure and power needed to make those choices.

04

Explore: What Is Environmental Health?

Environment

The environment includes natural and built surroundings, as well as the conditions in which people live, learn, work and travel.

Hazard

A hazard is something that has the potential to cause harm.

Hazard ≠ automatically the same as exposure.

Exposure

Exposure occurs when a person or population comes into contact with an environmental agent or condition.

Risk

Risk depends on the hazard, exposure, susceptibility and surrounding conditions.

Prevention

The strongest environmental-health response often reduces or removes harmful exposure before illness occurs.

Equity

Environmental health asks who is exposed, who is protected, who has control and who bears the greatest burden.

THINK LIKE A SCIENTIST

Before saying that something is dangerous, ask: What is the hazard? Who is exposed? How much? For how long? And what evidence supports the conclusion?

05

Learn: Major Environmental Health Risks

01

Air Pollution & Clean Air

Air pollution includes particulate matter and gases from sources such as transportation, energy production, industry, fires and household fuels.

WHO's 2026 evidence brief identifies air pollution as a leading environmental health risk. CDC also notes that climate change can increase ground-level ozone and particulate pollution in some locations.

Explore the dedicated Air Pollution module →
02

Water, Sanitation & Hygiene

Safe water, sanitation and hygiene are fundamental environmental conditions for health.

Contaminated water and inadequate sanitation can contribute to infectious disease, while reliable infrastructure supports dignity, education and healthy communities.

WHO identifies water, sanitation and hygiene among the major environmental determinants of health.

03

Climate Change & Heat

Climate change affects health through heat, air pollution, extreme weather, changing infectious-disease patterns, food and water systems and other pathways.

Health Canada identifies extreme heat and wildfire smoke among important climate-related health risks in Canada.

04

Chemicals & Hazardous Materials

People encounter chemicals through air, water, food, products and workplaces. Most everyday chemical exposure does not automatically mean harm, but some chemicals can affect health depending on their properties and exposure.

UNEP emphasizes that chemical pollution can affect people, animals and ecosystems and can contribute to major health problems.

05

Waste & E-Waste

Poorly managed waste can contaminate air, water and soil. Electronic waste can contain hazardous substances and creates environmental and health challenges when not managed appropriately.

Children are among the populations WHO identifies as particularly vulnerable to environmental risks associated with e-waste.

06

UV Radiation & Sun Exposure

Ultraviolet radiation is part of the natural environment. Excessive exposure can damage skin and eyes and increase the risk of skin cancer.

Environmental health therefore includes learning how to use shade, clothing and appropriate sun-protection measures.

07

Built Environments

Buildings, neighbourhood design, transportation, housing, green space and energy systems influence how people move, breathe, interact and access healthy resources.

A healthy built environment can make healthier choices easier and can reduce environmental exposure.

08

Biodiversity & One Health

Human, animal and environmental health are interconnected.

WHO's One Health approach recognizes that preventing disease and protecting health may require collaboration across human health, animal health and environmental systems.

06

Think: Who Is Most Exposed?

01

Who is exposed?

Identify the people, places and populations that may experience the environmental condition.

02

Who has least control?

Can people realistically change their housing, workplace, neighbourhood or access to services?

03

Who benefits?

Which economic, social or infrastructure systems create benefits — and who bears their costs?

04

Who can change the system?

Individuals matter, but so do schools, communities, businesses, municipalities and governments.

GHE EQUITY QUESTION

Is it fair to ask people to avoid an environmental hazard when they have little control over their exposure?

07

Healthy Environments: What Can We Do?

At home

  • Pay attention to reliable information about indoor air, heat and environmental hazards.
  • Keep indoor spaces appropriately ventilated when conditions allow.
  • Follow safety instructions for household products and environmental hazards.
  • Learn about local drinking-water information.

At school

  • Think about air quality, heat, shade and healthy learning spaces.
  • Learn how schools can prepare for wildfire smoke and extreme heat.
  • Reduce unnecessary waste and protect shared spaces.
  • Turn environmental questions into evidence-based projects.

In the community

  • Learn how local environmental conditions are monitored.
  • Explore transportation, green space and neighbourhood design.
  • Support clean and safe shared environments.
  • Ask whether environmental protection reaches all neighbourhoods fairly.

In society

  • Support evidence-based environmental policies.
  • Encourage clean energy and healthy transportation systems.
  • Protect water, ecosystems and biodiversity.
  • Connect environmental policy with health equity.
08

Social Determinants of Health & Environmental Equity

Environmental exposure is strongly connected to the conditions in which people live, learn, work and travel.

WHO's work on environmental health and the social determinants of health emphasizes the importance of addressing structural and social conditions, not only individual behaviour.

INCOME

Can influence housing, energy access, transportation and the ability to avoid environmental hazards.

HOUSING

Influences ventilation, dampness, mould, temperature and indoor environmental quality.

LOCATION

Neighbourhoods may differ in exposure to traffic, industry, heat, green space and environmental services.

WORK

Occupational environments can influence exposure to heat, dust, chemicals, noise and other hazards.

INFRASTRUCTURE

Water, sanitation, energy, transport and waste systems shape environmental health.

POWER & POLICY

Laws, regulations, planning and political decisions determine many environmental conditions.

HEALTH EQUITY

Environmental justice means considering whether everyone has a fair opportunity to live in a healthy environment — not simply whether everyone receives the same advice.

Environmental Health in Action

WHO

Develops health-based guidance, synthesizes evidence and supports countries on environmental risks including air pollution, climate, WASH and children's environmental health.

UNEP

Leads global environmental action and works across pollution, chemicals, climate, ecosystems and sustainable development.

CDC

Provides evidence and public-health guidance on environmental and climate-related risks, including heat and air pollution.

Health Canada

Provides Canadian evidence and guidance on air quality, climate change, heat, water, chemicals, sun safety and other environmental health issues.

Communities

Communities understand local conditions, identify concerns and can participate in environmental decision-making.

Young people

Young Global Health Explorers can ask better questions, interpret evidence and participate responsibly in improving the environments they share.

Test Your Thinking: 12 MCQs

These questions test environmental-health knowledge, application, equity, systems thinking, evidence and global-health reasoning.

1. What does environmental health study?

A. Only infectious diseases

B. How environmental conditions can affect health

C. Only hospital treatment

D. Only individual exercise

Answer: B Environmental health examines how physical, chemical, biological and built environments influence health.
2. What is the difference between a hazard and exposure?

A. They always mean exactly the same thing

B. A hazard has potential to cause harm; exposure means contact with it

C. Exposure can never affect health

D. A hazard only exists indoors

Answer: B Distinguishing hazard from exposure helps us reason more carefully about environmental risk.
3. Why are water, sanitation and hygiene important?

A. They have no relationship with health

B. They can help prevent disease and support healthy communities

C. They only matter in hospitals

D. They only affect adults

Answer: B WHO identifies safe water, sanitation and hygiene as major foundations of environmental health.
4. Which is an environmental health concern?

A. Air pollution

B. Extreme heat

C. Unsafe water

D. All of the above

Answer: D Environmental health includes many interacting risks rather than one single exposure.
5. Why can climate change become a health issue?

A. It can influence heat, air quality, extreme weather and other health pathways

B. Climate has no relationship with health

C. It only affects plants

D. It only affects oceans

Answer: A WHO, CDC and Health Canada describe multiple direct and indirect climate-health pathways.
6. Which statement best describes environmental equity?

A. Everyone experiences exactly the same exposure

B. Everyone should have a fair opportunity to live in a healthy environment

C. Environmental risks are always personal choices

D. Only governments are affected by pollution

Answer: B Equity asks whether environmental conditions, risks and protections are distributed fairly.
7. A neighbourhood has higher traffic pollution. What should a Global Health Explorer ask first?

A. Why don't residents simply move?

B. What evidence shows the exposure and what factors may explain it?

C. Whether pollution can be ignored

D. Whether measurement is unnecessary

Answer: B Evidence, exposure patterns and underlying conditions should be examined before drawing conclusions.
8. Why are children important in environmental-health research?

A. Children are never exposed to environmental risks

B. Development is ongoing and early exposures can influence health across the life course

C. Children are affected only by air pollution

D. Environmental risks affect only adults

Answer: B WHO identifies children as particularly vulnerable to several environmental risks.
9. Which approach best represents One Health?

A. Studying human health without considering animals or ecosystems

B. Connecting human, animal and environmental health

C. Studying only hospitals

D. Ignoring environmental change

Answer: B One Health recognizes the interconnectedness of people, animals and the environment.
10. Which SDG is directly connected to environmental health?

A. SDG 3 only

B. SDG 6 only

C. SDG 13 only

D. Multiple SDGs, including 3, 6, 7, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15

Answer: D Environmental health is inherently cross-sectoral and therefore connects many SDGs.
11. Which organizations contribute evidence and action on environmental health?

A. WHO

B. UNEP

C. CDC and Health Canada

D. All of the above

Answer: D Environmental health requires collaboration across international, national, local and community institutions.
12. Which response best represents the GHE approach?

A. Blame individuals for environmental risks

B. Ignore evidence and rely on opinion

C. Combine evidence, equity, personal awareness, community action and systems thinking

D. Treat environmental health as unrelated to global health

Answer: C GHE connects evidence with responsible action, moving from SELF to WORLD.
