Why is respiratory health important?

Every breath connects us to the world around us. Our lungs bring oxygen into the body and remove carbon dioxide. Healthy lungs are therefore essential for learning, playing, sleeping, working and living.

Respiratory health is affected by much more than individual behaviour. Tobacco smoke, household and outdoor air pollution, occupational exposures, infections, housing conditions and access to healthcare can all influence respiratory health.

Asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) are important chronic respiratory diseases. Respiratory infections can also cause substantial illness, particularly among young children, older adults and people whose health is already compromised.

Explore

Let's build a common language for understanding respiratory health.

Respiratory system

The organs and structures involved in breathing, including the airways and lungs.

Airway

A passage through which air travels into and out of the lungs.

Asthma

A chronic respiratory condition involving inflammation and narrowing of the airways that can cause symptoms such as wheezing, coughing and difficulty breathing.

COPD

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease is a chronic lung disease that causes persistent airflow limitation and breathing difficulties.

Air pollution

Contamination of indoor or outdoor air by substances that can harm human health or the environment.

Occupational exposure

Contact with potentially harmful substances or conditions during work.

04

Learn

How does breathing work?

When we breathe in, air travels through the nose or mouth, down the airways and into the lungs. Oxygen passes into the bloodstream, while carbon dioxide moves from the blood into the lungs and is breathed out.

The respiratory system works together with the cardiovascular system to deliver oxygen throughout the body.

Asthma

Asthma can affect people of all ages, including children and adolescents. Symptoms may include coughing, wheezing, chest tightness and difficulty breathing.

Symptoms can vary over time and may be triggered or worsened by factors such as allergens, smoke, air pollution, respiratory infections, exercise or cold air.

Asthma is manageable, and appropriate medical care can help people participate fully in school, sport and everyday life.

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease

COPD is a chronic respiratory disease that makes breathing increasingly difficult. Tobacco smoke is an important risk factor, but it is not the only one.

Long-term exposure to household air pollution, outdoor air pollution, dusts, fumes and chemicals can also contribute to respiratory disease.

Respiratory infections

Respiratory infections range from relatively mild illnesses to severe diseases such as pneumonia. Their impact can be greater where people have limited access to vaccination, clean air, healthcare, nutritious food or safe housing.

Public-health measures, vaccination where appropriate, infection prevention and timely access to care can reduce serious illness.

Tobacco and respiratory health

Tobacco smoke damages the respiratory system and is associated with multiple respiratory diseases, cardiovascular diseases and cancers.

Protecting respiratory health therefore includes both supporting people who use tobacco to quit and creating environments that reduce exposure to tobacco smoke.

Air pollution and the lungs

Fine particulate matter and other air pollutants can enter the respiratory system and contribute to respiratory and cardiovascular disease.

This makes clean air a population-health issue. Individual actions alone cannot solve pollution caused by transportation, industry, energy production, fires or other large-scale sources.

Social Determinants of Health

Respiratory health is strongly influenced by the conditions in which people live, learn, work and age.

  • Housing: overcrowding, dampness, mould, poor ventilation and indoor pollution can affect respiratory health.
  • Income: can influence housing quality, transportation, exposure to hazards and access to healthcare.
  • Work: dusts, fumes, chemicals and other occupational exposures can damage respiratory health.
  • Education: affects access to health information and the ability to navigate health services.
  • Environment: outdoor air pollution, wildfire smoke and climate- related hazards can affect breathing.
  • Healthcare access: determines whether people can obtain diagnosis, appropriate medicines and ongoing care.
Equity question:

Can we ask everyone to "breathe healthy air" when some people have far less control over the air surrounding them?

Global Leadership & Evidence

Who leads respiratory-health action?

  • WHO — global guidance on respiratory diseases, tobacco, air pollution and health systems.
  • WHO Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease partners — evidence and clinical approaches for COPD.
  • WHO Global Initiative for Asthma partners — evidence-based approaches to asthma care.
  • UN Environment Programme (UNEP) — environmental policies and action relevant to air quality.
  • National and provincial health authorities — surveillance, prevention, clinical services and public-health programmes.
  • Universities and scientific organizations — respiratory research, surveillance and innovation.
