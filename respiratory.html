01

Why is respiratory health important?

Breathing is something we usually notice only when it becomes difficult. Respiratory health affects our ability to learn, play, sleep, exercise and participate in everyday life.

The respiratory system brings oxygen into the body and removes carbon dioxide. It works closely with the cardiovascular system to deliver oxygen to tissues throughout the body.

Respiratory health is influenced by much more than individual choices. Tobacco smoke, household and outdoor air pollution, infections, allergens, occupational exposures, housing conditions, climate-related hazards and access to healthcare can all influence respiratory health.

Conditions such as asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) can affect people across the life course. Respiratory infections can also cause substantial illness, particularly among young children, older adults and people living with other health vulnerabilities.

03

Explore

Let's build a common language for understanding respiratory health.

Respiratory system

The organs and structures involved in breathing, including the airways and lungs.

Airway

A passage through which air travels into and out of the lungs.

Asthma

A chronic respiratory condition involving inflammation and narrowing of the airways that can cause symptoms such as wheezing, coughing and difficulty breathing.

COPD

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease is a chronic lung disease involving persistent airflow limitation and breathing difficulties.

Air pollution

Contamination of indoor or outdoor air by substances that can harm human health or the environment.

Occupational exposure

Contact with potentially harmful substances or conditions during work.

Ventilation

The movement and replacement of indoor air. Good ventilation can help improve indoor air quality.

Respiratory infection

An infection affecting parts of the respiratory system, ranging from mild illness to severe disease.

04

Learn

Understanding respiratory health means connecting biology, environment, prevention, healthcare and society.

How does breathing work?

When we breathe in, air travels through the nose or mouth, through the airways and into the lungs. Oxygen passes from the lungs into the bloodstream, while carbon dioxide moves from the blood into the lungs and is breathed out.

Breathing is therefore not only a lung function. The respiratory and cardiovascular systems work together to deliver oxygen throughout the body.

Asthma

Asthma can affect children, adolescents and adults. Symptoms can include coughing, wheezing, chest tightness and difficulty breathing.

Symptoms can vary over time and may be triggered or worsened by factors such as allergens, smoke, air pollution, respiratory infections, exercise or cold air.

Asthma can be managed. Appropriate care can help people participate fully in education, physical activity, relationships and everyday life.

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease

COPD is a chronic respiratory disease that can make breathing increasingly difficult.

Tobacco smoke is an important risk factor, but it is not the only one. Long-term exposure to household air pollution, outdoor air pollution, dusts, fumes and chemicals can also contribute to respiratory disease.

Respiratory infections

Respiratory infections range from relatively mild illnesses to severe diseases such as pneumonia.

Their effects can be influenced by vaccination access, living conditions, nutrition, indoor air quality, healthcare access and other social and environmental factors.

Prevention, appropriate vaccination where recommended, infection prevention and timely access to healthcare can reduce serious illness.

Tobacco and respiratory health

Tobacco smoke damages the respiratory system and is associated with respiratory diseases, cardiovascular diseases and several cancers.

Respiratory-health action therefore includes both supporting people who use tobacco to quit and creating environments that reduce exposure to tobacco smoke.

Air pollution and the lungs

Fine particulate matter and other air pollutants can affect the respiratory system and contribute to respiratory and cardiovascular disease.

Major sources can include transportation, industry, energy production, household fuels, agriculture and landscape fires. The importance of each source varies by place.

This makes clean air a population-health issue. Individual action alone cannot solve pollution generated by large-scale systems.

Climate change and respiratory health

Climate-related hazards can affect respiratory health. Wildfire smoke, extreme heat, changing allergen patterns and other environmental changes can create additional risks in some communities.

Climate action can therefore also be health action.

Social Determinants of Health

Where people live, learn, work and age can shape the air they breathe and the care they can access.

Respiratory health is influenced by social, economic, environmental and health-system conditions.

  • Housing: overcrowding, dampness, mould, poor ventilation and indoor pollution can affect respiratory health.
  • Income: can influence housing quality, transportation, exposure to hazards and access to healthcare.
  • Work: dusts, fumes, chemicals and other occupational exposures can damage respiratory health.
  • Education: can influence access to reliable health information and the ability to navigate health services.
  • Environment: outdoor air pollution, wildfire smoke and other climate-related hazards can affect breathing.
  • Healthcare access: determines whether people can obtain diagnosis, appropriate medicines and ongoing care.
  • Location and infrastructure: transportation systems, urban planning, industry and energy systems can influence exposure to air pollution.
Equity question

Can we ask everyone to "breathe healthy air" when some people have far less control over the air surrounding them?

Sustainable Development Goals

Respiratory health connects health with environmental, social, economic and climate goals.

SDG 3 Good Health & Well-being
SDG 7 Affordable & Clean Energy
SDG 10 Reduced Inequalities

SDG 12 Responsible Consumption & Production

SDG 3 is the most direct connection, but respiratory health also depends on clean energy, sustainable cities, reduced inequality, responsible production and climate action.

This illustrates an important global-health principle: health outcomes are often produced by systems far beyond the health sector itself.

09

Global Leadership & Evidence

Who leads respiratory-health action?

  • World Health Organization (WHO) — develops global guidance and monitors respiratory diseases, air pollution, tobacco and health-system responses.
  • UN Environment Programme (UNEP) — works on environmental policy and action relevant to air quality.
  • National, provincial and local health authorities — conduct surveillance, prevention, health promotion and healthcare delivery.
  • Environmental and occupational authorities — regulate and monitor environmental and workplace exposures.
  • Scientific and professional organizations — generate evidence, clinical guidance and research on respiratory diseases.
  • Universities and research institutions — study respiratory disease, environmental exposures, prevention, treatment and health equity.
GHE evidence principle:

Global health knowledge is produced by many partners. Young people should learn not only what the evidence says, but also who produces it, how it is monitored and why evidence may change over time.

12

Act

I can make healthy choices for myself, and I can help create healthier conditions for others.
  • Learn how to identify reliable respiratory-health information.
  • Support smoke-free environments and respect the health of people affected by second-hand smoke.
  • Learn how local air quality is monitored and communicated.
  • Help reduce stigma toward people living with asthma, COPD or other respiratory conditions.
  • Explore how schools, homes and public buildings can support healthier indoor environments.
  • Investigate how transportation, energy and urban policies affect the air people breathe.
  • Ask how respiratory healthcare can become more accessible and equitable.
  • Use evidence to participate constructively in conversations about clean air and environmental health.
