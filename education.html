🌍 EXPLORE THE WORLD · EDUCATION

How are learning and opportunity distributed?

Education can open pathways to knowledge, health, participation and opportunity. But access to school does not automatically mean access to learning.

Explore what global data reveal about who gets to school, who completes it, what children learn, and how differences in wealth, gender, geography and other circumstances shape educational opportunity.

🏫 SDG 4 Quality Education

Inclusive and equitable quality education and lifelong learning opportunities for all.

WHY EDUCATION MATTERS

Education is more than going to school.

A global education system can be explored through several connected questions: Can children enter school? Do they remain in school? Do they complete their education? Are they learning? Do they have qualified teachers and safe learning environments? And are opportunities distributed fairly?

💡

GHE principle: Being counted in school is important — but learning, opportunity and inclusion matter too.

LOOK CLOSER

Access to school is not the same as learning.

Imagine two places where nearly every child attends school. Their education systems might still produce very different learning outcomes.

Attendance tells us whether children are participating in education. Completion tells us whether they progress through a level. Learning indicators ask a different question: what knowledge and skills are children actually developing?

EQUITY

The average can hide the difference.

A national education average can look encouraging while important groups remain behind.

UNICEF and UNESCO emphasize the importance of examining education by factors such as household wealth, gender, geography, disability and other circumstances.

GHE explorers should therefore ask:

  • Who is included in the average?
  • Who may be missing?
  • Which groups experience lower access or learning?
  • Does the gap change over time?

THE PEOPLE WHO MAKE LEARNING POSSIBLE

Education systems depend on teachers.

Buildings, technology and textbooks matter — but learning also depends on people.

82%

Professional qualification

Globally, about 82% of primary teachers meet professional qualification requirements.

Qualification standards differ between countries, so percentages should always be interpreted in context.

UNESCO reports that teacher qualification gaps are particularly important in low-income settings. This reminds us that an education indicator should be connected to the wider education system.

WHERE LEARNING HAPPENS

A school is more than a classroom.

Safe and supportive learning environments are part of education quality. Water, sanitation, hygiene, electricity, safety and protection can all influence the conditions in which students learn.

💧

Water

Access to safe drinking water is part of a healthy learning environment.

🧼

Hygiene

Handwashing facilities can support health and a safer school environment.

⚡

Electricity

Reliable infrastructure can influence what learning resources schools can use.

🛡️

Safety

Students need learning environments that are safe, inclusive and free from violence.

A useful GHE question: What conditions around a learner might shape their ability to learn?

EDUCATION IN A DIGITAL WORLD

Technology can expand learning — but it does not replace good teaching.

UNESCO's work on technology in education emphasizes that digital tools should support learners and teachers rather than substitute for human interaction.

Digital access also creates a new data question: who has the opportunity to use technology meaningfully?

EDUCATION & THE SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT GOALS

Quality education is a global commitment.

Sustainable Development Goal 4 calls for inclusive and equitable quality education and lifelong learning opportunities for all.

Education also connects with many other Sustainable Development Goals — including health, gender equality, poverty reduction, decent work, reduced inequalities, climate action and peace.

GHE DATA SKILL

Before comparing education data, ask six questions.

01 What is being measured?
02 Who is being measured?
03 What year is the data from?
04 What is the denominator?
05 Are the places comparable?
06 Who produced the data?
🔎

Remember: A percentage is not a conclusion. It is evidence that needs context.
