THE GHE RESPONSIBLE INVESTIGATOR

Ask boldly. Investigate responsibly.

GHE encourages young people to become more than observers of global health. A responsible investigator asks meaningful questions, looks carefully at evidence, listens to affected people, recognizes uncertainty and thinks about who may benefit from the work.

You do not need to know everything before you begin. Responsible investigation means learning as you go, seeking guidance when needed and being honest about what the evidence can—and cannot—tell you.

YOUR IDEA CAN START HERE

Build Your GHE Mini-Proposal

Every meaningful project begins with a question. You do not need a finished research paper. Start with curiosity, examine evidence, listen to people and describe what you would like to explore or change.

10
Ten questions can turn an idea into a pathway.

Work through the steps below. Your first version can be short. You can improve it as you learn.

01

The Question

What are you curious about?

What have you noticed? What do you want to understand better? What question keeps coming back to you?
02

The Problem

Why does it matter?

Who may be affected? Where does the issue occur? What might happen if the problem continues? Why is it important now?
03

The Evidence

What do we already know?

What research, reports, observations or reliable information have you found? What remains uncertain?
04

The People

Who is affected?

Which people or communities experience the issue? Whose voices should be heard? Who should be involved?
05

Your Idea

What would you like to investigate, develop or change?

Describe your proposed investigation, project, innovation, educational activity or possible intervention.
06

The Data

What datasets have you reviewed?

Which relevant sources have you explored, such as WHO, IHME, UNICEF, UN, UNEP or other reliable sources? What did your analysis suggest?
07

Your Team

Who will work with you?

Who are your teammates? What strengths, skills or perspectives does each person bring to the investigation?
08

Teacher / Mentor Approval

Who will provide guidance?

Identify the teacher, mentor or responsible adult who can supervise and support the project where appropriate.
09

The Beneficiary

Who could benefit?

Could the work benefit an individual, family, school, local community, municipality, public agency, national organization or international organization?
10

The Next Step

What support do you need?

What collaboration, mentorship, data, expertise, equipment, community connection or other support would help you move forward?
