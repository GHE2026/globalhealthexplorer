Injury Prevention

Injuries can happen at home, at school, during physical activity, while travelling, or in other everyday settings. Injury prevention helps us recognize hazards, understand risk, make thoughtful choices, and contribute to safer environments for ourselves and others.

PERSONAL CARE · PAGE 05

Injury Prevention belongs to the Personal Care pathway.

Personal care is not only about what we do for ourselves. It also includes recognizing hazards, preparing for everyday situations, protecting ourselves and others, and helping create safer homes, schools, communities, and environments.

Explore Personal Care Hub →
WHY THIS MATTERS

Prevention starts before an injury happens.

Injury prevention is about understanding hazards, identifying situations that increase risk, and taking reasonable steps to make everyday activities safer.

01

Recognize Hazards

A hazard is something that can contribute to harm. Learning to notice hazards is an important first step in prevention.

02

Understand Risk

Risk can change depending on the person, activity, environment, equipment, and circumstances.

03

Build Safer Environments

Prevention is stronger when individuals, families, schools, communities, and systems work together.

PREVENTION IN EVERYDAY LIFE

Look ahead. Notice what could make a situation less safe.

Many injuries occur in ordinary environments. Prevention therefore begins with everyday awareness rather than waiting for something to go wrong.

  • Notice hazards before beginning an activity.
  • Use appropriate safety equipment and follow instructions.
  • Consider the environment as well as the activity.
  • Speak up when something does not seem safe.
  • Know when to stop and ask for help.

Four habits that support prevention

01

03

04

LEARN

Understanding injury prevention

01

Injuries have multiple causes.

An injury may be influenced by individual behaviour, the environment, equipment, infrastructure, social conditions, or several factors together.

02

Risk depends on context.

The same activity can carry different levels of risk depending on where, when, how, and by whom it is performed.

03

Prevention can occur at many levels.

Individuals can make safer choices, while families, schools, communities, governments, and organizations can improve environments and systems.

04

Safety is a shared responsibility.

A safer environment is rarely created by one person alone. Prevention often depends on cooperation, communication, design, policy, and evidence.

05

Asking for help is part of prevention.

Recognizing uncertainty and seeking appropriate guidance is a responsible safety behaviour.

THINK

Good prevention asks not only “What happened?” but also “Why was this situation possible?”

01

02

03

04

Shared Safety

SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Safety is shaped by the world around us.

People do not experience the same risks or have the same opportunities to prevent injury. Conditions in which people live, learn, work, travel, and grow matter.

Housing

Safe, stable housing can influence exposure to hazards and opportunities for prevention.

Education

Knowledge and safety education can influence awareness, confidence, and decision-making.

Transportation

Roads, public transport, vehicles, and infrastructure all influence injury risk.

Environment

Physical environments can either reduce or increase hazards.

Access to Services

Prevention and timely support depend partly on access to appropriate services and information.

Equity

Effective prevention should consider who is most exposed to risk and who has the fewest resources to reduce it.

PREVENTION IS A SYSTEM

Safer choices are important — but safer systems matter too.

Injury prevention should not place all responsibility on individuals. Good design, safe infrastructure, clear information, appropriate standards, and effective policies can make safer choices easier.

This is an important global health lesson: prevention is both personal and structural.
