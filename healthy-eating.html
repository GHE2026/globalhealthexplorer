WHY IS THIS IMPORTANT?

Food is fundamental to health.

Food supports growth, development, learning, energy and wellbeing. A healthy diet is generally understood through the principles of adequacy, balance, moderation and diversity.

Healthy dietary patterns developed during childhood and adolescence can contribute to health across the life course.

But healthy eating is not simply a matter of knowing what is nutritious. Food choices and opportunities are shaped by family, culture, income, time, school, food environments, marketing and the wider food system.

TWO QUESTIONS MATTER
How can I make informed choices for my own health?
How can we create conditions in which everyone has a fair opportunity to eat well?

START WITH YOURSELF

🌱 Personal Health Hints

Healthy eating is a pattern, not a test. Use these questions to notice your habits, environment and opportunities without judging yourself.

01

Variety

Do I eat a variety of foods? Different foods can provide different nutrients.

02

Balance

Does my usual food pattern include a useful balance of carbohydrates, protein, fats, fibre, vitamins and minerals?

03

Fibre

Do I regularly eat fibre-rich foods such as vegetables, fruits, whole grains, beans, lentils, nuts and seeds?

04

Food Processing

How often do highly processed foods become the easiest option? Convenience matters — so does the overall pattern.

05

Sugar

How often do foods or drinks high in free sugar become part of my usual pattern?

06

Regular Opportunities

Do I usually have reasonable opportunities to eat? School, activities and family circumstances matter.

07

Energy & Concentration

Do I notice how different foods and eating patterns affect my energy and concentration?

08

Food & Feelings

How can stress, boredom, celebrations or emotions influence eating?

09

Food Environment

How easy is it for me to access nutritious food? What makes it easier or harder?

10

One Small Improvement

What is one realistic, positive change I could try?

💚 Remember

Healthy eating is not about perfection, restriction, body shape, appearance or comparing yourself with others.

It is about nourishment, informed habits, enjoyment, culture and family traditions — while recognizing that circumstances and access matter.

LEARN

Building Nutrition Literacy

Understanding food means looking beyond individual foods to nutrients, patterns, environments, systems and evidence.

Healthy Diet

Adequacy, balance, moderation and diversity.

Nutrients

Carbohydrates, protein, fats, fibre, vitamins, minerals and water.

Food Patterns

Vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds and nutritious protein sources.

Food Processing

Understand different levels of processing without simplistic “good” and “bad” labels.

Food Security

Reliable access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food.

Malnutrition

Different forms of inadequate nutrition can affect people across the life course.

Food Safety

Safe food handling, preparation and storage matter.

Food Environment

Availability, affordability, accessibility, marketing and social context shape opportunities.

SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Is Healthy Eating Only About Choice?

Our opportunities to eat well are influenced by the conditions around us.

LOCAL

What surrounds us?

  • Household income
  • Food prices
  • School food
  • Transportation
  • Neighbourhood access
  • Family circumstances
NATIONAL

What shapes systems?

  • Poverty and inequality
  • Social protection
  • Food policy
  • Agricultural policy
  • Education
  • Food marketing
GLOBAL

What connects the world?

  • Food systems
  • Conflict
  • Climate change
  • Food-price shocks
  • Displacement
  • Global inequality
HEALTH IS SHAPED BY CONDITIONS — NOT ONLY BY CHOICES.

GHE DATA LITERACY

Don't Just Accept the Number

🌍 THE CHALLENGE

A statistic is the beginning of a question — not the end.

Global estimates show that many people cannot afford a healthy diet. But what does that number actually tell us?

01 What does “cannot afford” actually mean?
02 Is this the same as being hungry?
03 How is it measured?
04 Which populations might be most affected?
05 Has the situation changed over time?
06 Which SDGs are involved?
07 Who collected the evidence?
GHE EVIDENCE LITERACY PRINCIPLE

Never accept a statistic without asking: Who measured it? When? How? Compared with what?
