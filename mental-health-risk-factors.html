MENTAL HEALTH

What Can Affect Mental Health?

Mental health is shaped by many things — our biology, experiences, relationships, environments and opportunities. Understanding risk factors helps us recognize challenges earlier and build healthier communities.

Mental Health Hub → Explore Protective Factors
WHY IS THIS IMPORTANT?

Risk factors are not destiny.

A risk factor is something that may increase the likelihood of a health problem. It does not mean that a person will develop that problem.

Many factors interact

Mental health is influenced by biological, psychological, social and environmental factors. These factors can interact throughout life rather than acting independently.

People respond differently

Two people can experience similar circumstances and have different responses. Personal strengths, relationships, access to support and wider social conditions all matter.

EXPLORE

Where do risk factors come from?

Think of mental health as the result of many influences operating together.

01

Biological factors

Genetics, brain development, physical health, sleep, hormones and other biological processes can influence mental health.

02

Psychological factors

Coping patterns, prolonged stress, emotional difficulties, low self-confidence and previous experiences can influence wellbeing.

03

Social factors

Relationships, loneliness, bullying, discrimination, social exclusion and lack of supportive networks can affect mental health.

04

Environmental factors

Housing, education, neighbourhood conditions, pollution, conflict, displacement and access to services can shape mental health.

LEARN

Risk factors can change across the life course.

Different stages of life bring different experiences, responsibilities, opportunities and challenges.

CHILDHOOD

Early experiences

Safe relationships, supportive caregiving, learning environments and opportunities to play and develop are important for healthy development.

ADOLESCENCE

A time of major change

Adolescence involves important biological, emotional and social changes. Peer relationships, school pressures, identity, discrimination and social environments can influence wellbeing.

ADULTHOOD

Work, relationships and responsibilities

Employment, financial security, relationships, caregiving, social connection and physical health can influence mental wellbeing.

LATER LIFE

Connection and changing circumstances

Social connection, physical health, independence, bereavement, financial security and access to appropriate services can become especially important.

THINK

Stress is part of life. Chronic stress is different.

Short periods of stress can be a normal response to challenges. Problems may arise when stress becomes prolonged, overwhelming or difficult to manage.

The effects of chronic stress can involve sleep, concentration, mood, relationships and physical health. Supportive environments and effective coping strategies can help reduce its impact.

EQUITY • SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Mental health is also about the conditions in which people live.

Health opportunities are not distributed equally. Understanding equity means asking why some people face greater barriers than others.

Income & poverty

Financial insecurity can affect housing, food security, education, safety and access to health services.

Education

Educational opportunities and supportive learning environments can influence health, confidence and future opportunities.

Discrimination

Racism, stigma, exclusion and other forms of discrimination can create additional stress and barriers to support.

Housing & safety

Safe, stable housing and safe communities are important foundations for physical and mental wellbeing.

Access to care

Distance, cost, availability, language and cultural safety can all influence whether people can obtain appropriate support.

Social connection

Supportive relationships can protect wellbeing, while loneliness and social exclusion can increase vulnerability.

!
BREAKING STIGMA

Mental health problems are health problems — not character flaws.

Stigma can lead people to hide difficulties, feel ashamed or avoid seeking support. It can also influence how families, schools, workplaces and health systems respond.

Learning accurate information, using respectful language and listening without judgment are important ways to challenge stigma.

CONNECT

From SELF to WORLD

Mental health becomes a global-health issue when we connect individual experiences with the environments and systems around us.

SELF

What can I notice?

Notice changes in mood, stress, sleep, concentration, relationships and wellbeing.

FAMILY & PEERS

How can we support one another?

Listen, communicate respectfully and encourage people to seek appropriate support when needed.

COMMUNITY

What environments help?

Schools, neighbourhoods, sports, youth organizations and community services can promote belonging and wellbeing.

COUNTRY

Are opportunities equitable?

Policies and health systems can reduce barriers and improve access to prevention, care and social support.

WORLD

What shapes global mental health?

Conflict, displacement, climate change, inequality, migration, digital environments and global health policies can influence mental wellbeing.

PREVENTION & EARLY SUPPORT

What can we do?

Prevention is not about eliminating every risk. It is about strengthening protective factors, reducing avoidable risks and making support easier to access.

  • Build supportive relationships.
  • Develop healthy coping skills.
  • Support regular sleep and physical activity.
  • Reduce stigma and discrimination.
  • Create safe and inclusive learning environments.
  • Seek appropriate help when difficulties persist.
