EXPLORE THE WORLD

The world is full of stories. Data helps us discover them.

Around the world, people and communities experience different health, social, economic and environmental conditions. Data helps us see those differences — and thoughtful exploration helps us understand them.

A GHE QUESTION

What can a number, a map or a difference teach us about the world?

Look carefully. Ask questions. Investigate.
WHY EXPLORE THE WORLD?

The world is not one average.

Countries are different. Communities are different. People live in different environments and have different opportunities to stay healthy.

Global health data can help us recognize these differences. But a number alone cannot tell the whole story. We need definitions, context, methods, comparisons and questions.

The goal is not simply to know more numbers. The goal is to learn how to think with evidence.

01

See the difference

Data can reveal differences between countries, regions, communities and groups.

02

Understand the evidence

Ask what is being measured, who is included, when it was measured and how the estimate was made.

03

Ask better questions

A difference is a starting point for investigation, not automatically an explanation or a solution.

Geography can make patterns visible.

Maps help us see where differences occur. They help us notice patterns — but they do not automatically explain why those patterns exist.

What does a map add?

A table gives us values. A map can help us see geographic patterns, concentrations, gradients and differences between places.

Before interpreting a map, ask five questions.

WHAT?

Which indicator is shown?

WHO?

Which population is represented?

WHEN?

Which year or period?

HOW?

Observed, estimated or modelled?

CERTAINTY?

How strong or uncertain is the evidence?

CONNECT THE EVIDENCE

From difference to understanding.

When learners discover a difference between places or populations, they can connect the evidence to social determinants of health, environmental conditions, health systems and the Sustainable Development Goals.

The question is not simply: “Who is higher or lower?”

Ask: “What conditions might help us understand this pattern?”

QUICK KNOWLEDGE CHECK

Think like an explorer.

Choose the best answer, then explain why.

Q1 A country has a higher health rate than another. What should you do first?
Best approach: Check the indicator definition, population, year, source and comparability before explaining the difference.
Q2 What can a map show particularly well?
Best answer: Geographic patterns, concentrations, gradients and differences between places.
Q3 Does a geographic pattern automatically explain its cause?
No. A map can reveal where a pattern occurs. Additional evidence is needed to investigate why it occurs.
Q4 Why should you check uncertainty?
Because: Estimates and models can have uncertainty. Understanding that uncertainty helps learners interpret evidence responsibly.
Q5 What does “Every difference matters” mean?
It means: Differences are worth investigating. It does not mean that every difference has the same explanation or automatically represents unfairness.
Q6 What should come before proposing a solution?
Evidence and understanding. First investigate the pattern, context, evidence and possible determinants.
