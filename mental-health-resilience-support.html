EXPLORE

What Helps People Thrive?

Mental health is influenced by many factors. Some are within individuals, while others exist in families, schools, communities and society.

01

Resilience

Resilience is the capacity to adapt to challenges and recover or move forward after difficult experiences. It is not about never struggling.

02

Social Connection

Supportive relationships with family, friends, peers, teachers and communities can strengthen wellbeing and reduce isolation.

03

Healthy Environments

Safe schools, inclusive communities, meaningful participation and opportunities to belong can contribute to better mental health.

04

Access to Support

People should be able to obtain appropriate information, trusted support and professional care when they need it.

LEARN

Resilience Can Be Strengthened

Resilience is not simply an individual personality trait. It can develop through supportive relationships, positive experiences, skills, opportunities and environments.

A resilient approach does not mean expecting people to cope with everything alone. Sometimes resilience means recognizing that support is needed and knowing where to find it.

  • Building supportive relationships
  • Developing healthy routines
  • Learning how to manage stress
  • Developing problem-solving skills
  • Asking for help when challenges become difficult
  • Creating safe and inclusive environments
+

A useful question

Instead of asking only, "What's wrong?" also ask:

"What helps this person feel safe, connected, understood and supported?"

HELP-SEEKING

You Don't Have to Figure Everything Out Alone

If emotional or psychological difficulties are interfering with everyday life, school, relationships or participation, talking with a trusted adult or qualified professional can be an important step.

Depending on the situation, support might come from a parent, caregiver, teacher, school counsellor, family doctor, psychologist, social worker or another qualified professional.

Seeking support early can make it easier to understand what is happening and identify appropriate next steps.

THINK

Mental Health Stigma Can Become Another Barrier

Stigma can involve stereotypes, prejudice, discrimination or the belief that people experiencing mental health difficulties are somehow weak or responsible for their condition.

These attitudes can discourage people from talking about their experiences or seeking support.

Challenge stigma: Learn accurately. Listen respectfully. Avoid labels. Include people. Speak up when stereotypes cause harm.
MENTAL HEALTH PROMOTION

From Individual Wellbeing to Healthy Communities

Mental health promotion goes beyond treating disorders. It also asks how schools, workplaces, families, communities and societies can create conditions in which people can thrive.

Safe & Inclusive Schools

Belonging, respectful relationships, meaningful participation and supportive learning environments matter.

Healthy Communities

Community spaces, recreation, culture, social participation and accessible services can support wellbeing.

Equity & Social Justice

Mental health is shaped by income, housing, education, discrimination, safety, access to services and other social determinants of health.

Youth Participation

Young people should have opportunities to contribute to decisions that affect their lives, schools and communities.

PSYCHOLOGICAL MEASUREMENTS

Can Mental Health Be Measured?

Researchers and health professionals use questionnaires and other standardized tools to understand symptoms, wellbeing, functioning and changes over time.

These tools can support research, education and professional assessment. They are not a diagnosis by themselves.

WELLBEING

WHO-5 Well-Being Index

A brief measure of subjective psychological wellbeing used in research and health settings.

Learn about WHO-5 →
MOOD

PHQ-9

A widely used questionnaire for assessing depressive symptoms in clinical and research settings.

Professional interpretation is important. It should not be used by young people to diagnose themselves.

ANXIETY

GAD-7

A standardized questionnaire commonly used to assess symptoms associated with generalized anxiety.

A questionnaire score is not the same as a diagnosis.
