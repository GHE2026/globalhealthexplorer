RESILIENCE

Resilience does not mean never struggling.

Resilience is the capacity to adapt, recover and continue developing in the face of challenges. It is influenced by personal skills, relationships, resources and environments.

01

Connect

Supportive relationships can provide belonging, encouragement and practical help.

02

Adapt

Flexible thinking, problem-solving and healthy coping strategies can help people respond to challenges.

03

Seek support

Reaching out is not failure. Knowing when and where to seek appropriate support is an important life skill.

HEALTHY COPING

Small actions can support wellbeing.

No single strategy works for everyone. Healthy coping involves finding approaches that are safe, realistic and appropriate to the situation.

01

Talk to someone

A trusted friend, family member, teacher or other trusted adult can provide connection and perspective.

02

Mindful breathing

Simple breathing or attention exercises can help some people pause and reconnect with the present moment.

03

Move regularly

Regular physical activity supports overall physical and mental wellbeing.

04

Protect sleep

Consistent sleep routines can support learning, mood and everyday functioning.

05

Stay connected

Maintaining supportive relationships can reduce isolation and strengthen belonging.

06

Seek appropriate help

When difficulties persist or interfere with daily life, talking with an appropriate professional can be an important next step.

HELP-SEEKING

Asking for help is a strength.

People do not need to solve every mental-health challenge alone. A trusted adult, teacher, school counsellor, family member, healthcare professional or appropriate community service may be able to help.

Supporting a friend does not mean becoming their therapist. Listen, care and help connect them with appropriate support.

MEASURE TO UNDERSTAND

Mental Health & Psychological Tools

Psychological tools can help us understand wellbeing, personality, experiences and symptoms. But a questionnaire is only one piece of information.

01

Understand Yourself

Some tools explore personality, strengths, wellbeing or other aspects of individual differences.

02

Understand Symptoms

Some validated questionnaires are designed to identify symptoms that may warrant further attention.

03

Understand Measurement

Good measurement requires attention to reliability, validity, context, culture and purpose.

SELF-UNDERSTANDING

Big Five Personality Inventory

Personality is different from mental illness. The Big Five model describes broad dimensions of individual differences in personality.

It can be useful for learning about personality and self-understanding, but it is not a diagnostic test for mental health conditions.

MEASUREMENT LITERACY

Ask four questions before trusting a score.

R

Reliability

Does the tool produce reasonably consistent results?

V

Validity

Does it actually measure what it claims to measure?

C

Context

Is the tool appropriate for this population, culture, setting and purpose?

D

Decision

What should—and should not—be concluded from the result?

Measure to understand — not to label.

A questionnaire can provide useful information. It should not automatically be used to diagnose yourself or someone else.

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

10 Questions — Resilience, Support & Tools

1. What does resilience mean?

Correct answer: B. The capacity to adapt, recover and continue developing when facing challenges.

2. Does resilience mean never struggling?

Correct answer: C. No. Resilience does not eliminate difficulties.

3. Which is an appropriate way to support a friend?

Correct answer: A. Listen respectfully and help connect the person with an appropriate trusted adult or professional when needed.

4. What is an important limitation of a questionnaire?

Correct answer: D. A questionnaire does not automatically establish a diagnosis.

5. What does the Big Five model describe?

Correct answer: B. Broad dimensions of individual differences in personality.

6. Is the Big Five a diagnostic test for mental illness?

Correct answer: A. No. It is a personality framework, not a diagnostic test for mental-health conditions.

7. What does validity ask?

Correct answer: C. Whether a tool measures what it claims to measure.

8. Why does culture matter in psychological measurement?

Correct answer: D. Psychological concepts and measurement can be influenced by cultural and social context.

9. What is the best reason to seek professional support?

Correct answer: B. Persistent difficulties or changes that affect wellbeing, relationships, learning or daily functioning may warrant appropriate professional support.

10. What is the key GHE message about psychological tools?

Correct answer: A. Use tools to learn and understand—not to label or self-diagnose.
