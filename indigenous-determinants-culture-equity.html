EXPLORE

Indigenous health cannot be understood without context.

Indigenous peoples are diverse. There is no single Indigenous culture, health system or experience. Different peoples and communities have their own histories, languages, traditions, governance systems, relationships with land and approaches to health and wellbeing.

01

Culture

Culture can shape understandings of health, family, community, healing, identity, responsibility and wellbeing.

02

Context

Historical and present-day social, economic and environmental conditions can influence opportunities for health.

03

Equity

Equity asks whether people have fair opportunities to achieve health and whether barriers created by systems and environments are being addressed.

UNDERSTAND

Culture, Identity & Wellbeing

Culture can be an important source of identity, belonging, continuity, knowledge and strength. It should not be treated as a stereotype or as something identical across all Indigenous peoples.

ID

Identity

A strong sense of identity and belonging can contribute to wellbeing and connection.

LN

Language

Indigenous languages can carry knowledge, relationships, history and cultural meaning across generations.

FM

Family & Community

Relationships, collective responsibilities and community connections may be important dimensions of wellbeing.

LD

Land & Place

Relationships with land, water and place can have cultural, social, spiritual and health significance.

Important:

Indigenous peoples are not one group. GHE uses the term “Indigenous peoples” while recognizing the distinct identities, histories, rights, cultures and experiences of different nations and communities.

CONNECT

From SELF to WORLD

Indigenous health can be understood across interconnected levels rather than through an individual lens alone.

01

SELF

Identity, physical and mental wellbeing, personal experiences, language, beliefs, strengths and access to opportunities.

02

FAMILY & PEERS

Family relationships, peer networks, intergenerational knowledge, belonging, support and cultural continuity.

03

COMMUNITY

Community leadership, schools, housing, local services, safety, culture, language and connection to land.

04

COUNTRY

Public policies, health and education systems, economic opportunity, social protection, recognition of rights and equitable access to services.

05

WORLD

Global Indigenous rights, climate change, displacement, international inequalities, knowledge exchange and Indigenous leadership.

EQUITY • SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Health is shaped by the conditions in which people live.

The Social Determinants of Health lens helps us understand why health outcomes can differ between populations and why healthcare alone cannot eliminate health inequities.

Housing

Safe, adequate and affordable housing can influence physical and mental wellbeing.

Education

Equitable access to culturally relevant and high-quality education can shape lifelong opportunities.

Income & Employment

Economic security affects resources, choices and exposure to health risks.

Food & Nutrition

Access to nutritious, culturally appropriate food is an important determinant of health.

Environment

Water, land, pollution, climate and environmental change can influence health and wellbeing.

Health Services

Availability, affordability, accessibility and cultural safety influence whether people can obtain appropriate care.

Discrimination

Racism and discrimination can affect health directly and through barriers to opportunity and services.

Governance & Voice

Participation in decisions affecting communities can influence autonomy, trust and health-related outcomes.

THINK

Why does history matter for health?

In many regions, Indigenous peoples have experienced colonization, dispossession, forced displacement, suppression of languages and cultures, discrimination and other forms of structural injustice.

These experiences can have effects across generations through changes in family structures, cultural continuity, economic opportunity, trust in institutions and access to services.

Understanding history does not mean defining Indigenous peoples by historical harm. It helps explain present-day conditions and the importance of Indigenous leadership, rights, healing and self-determination.

THINK ABOUT IT

History → Systems → Opportunities → Health

Ask how historical decisions may have shaped today's institutions, environments and opportunities.

EQUITY

Racism and discrimination are health issues.

Discrimination can influence health through stress, exclusion, reduced opportunities and barriers to education, employment and healthcare.

01

Individual experiences

A person may experience prejudice or discrimination in everyday interactions.

02

Institutional barriers

Policies and organizational practices can unintentionally or intentionally create unequal access or outcomes.

03

Structural conditions

Wider systems of power and resource distribution can influence opportunities across populations.

Equity is not sameness.

Treating everyone identically does not necessarily produce equitable outcomes. Equity recognizes different starting conditions and works to remove unfair barriers.

CONNECT TO CARE

What does culturally safe care mean?

Culturally safe care aims to create healthcare environments where people are treated with dignity and respect and do not experience discrimination because of culture or identity.

01

Listen

Listen to patients, families and communities rather than assuming that one approach fits everyone.

02

Respect

Respect cultural identity, language, values and different understandings of health and wellbeing.

03

Reflect

Health professionals should reflect on their own assumptions, power and potential biases.

04

Partner

Meaningful partnership with Indigenous peoples and communities is essential to equitable health approaches.

Indigenous health and equity connect with multiple dimensions of sustainable development.

Addressing discrimination and unequal opportunities is central to health equity.

Quality Education

SDG 6

Safe water and sanitation are fundamental determinants of health.

SDG 11

Sustainable Communities

Safe, inclusive and resilient communities support wellbeing and participation.

SDG 13

SDG 16

Rights, participation, accountability and inclusive institutions are important to equity.

SDG 17

Partnerships

Equitable partnerships can support Indigenous-led solutions and knowledge exchange.

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

10 Indigenous Health Questions

These questions test understanding of culture, determinants, equity, history, culturally safe care and the GHE global-health perspective.

1. Why is it important not to treat Indigenous peoples as one homogeneous group?

Correct answer: B. Indigenous peoples represent many distinct nations, communities, cultures, languages, histories and contemporary experiences.

2. Which is an example of a Social Determinant of Health?

Correct answer: C. Housing, education, income, environment and access to services can all influence health and wellbeing.

3. Why can language be relevant to Indigenous health?

Correct answer: A. Language can carry identity, knowledge, culture, relationships and connections between generations.

4. Which statement best describes health equity?

Correct answer: D. Equity focuses on fair opportunities for health and addressing unfair barriers and differences in starting conditions.

5. Why does history matter when studying Indigenous health?

Correct answer: B. Historical processes can shape present-day systems, opportunities, environments, relationships and access to resources.

6. Which approach best supports culturally safe care?

Correct answer: C. Listening, respecting cultural identity, reflecting on assumptions and working meaningfully with Indigenous peoples can support culturally safe care.

7. Which question best reflects an equity lens?

Correct answer: A. Asking who faces greater barriers and who has access to protective resources helps identify inequities.

8. Why should Indigenous people be involved in decisions about Indigenous health?

Correct answer: D. Meaningful participation supports relevance, respect, self-determination, accountability and community ownership of health approaches.

9. Which SDG is most directly connected to health equity?

Correct answer: B. SDG 10 — Reduced Inequalities is particularly relevant, while SDG 3 and several other SDGs are also closely connected.

10. Which action best reflects the GHE approach?

Correct answer: C. Learn, listen, respect Indigenous knowledge and leadership, and support meaningful partnerships rather than assuming that outsiders know what a community needs.
