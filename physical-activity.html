HEALTHY LIVING · SUBJECT 2

Physical Activity
& Active Living

Moving, playing, learning and living actively—from ourselves to healthier families, communities and societies.

GLOBAL EVIDENCE

What does WHO recommend?

60 minutes

Average daily physical activity

For children and adolescents aged 5–17 years, WHO recommends an average of at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity per day across the week.

Most activity should be aerobic. Vigorous-intensity aerobic activities, as well as activities that strengthen muscles and bones, should be incorporated at least 3 days per week.

Every move counts.

Physical activity can include active play, walking, cycling, sports, recreation, physical education, transportation and many forms of everyday movement.

EXPLORE

Why is this important?

Physical activity is an important part of healthy growth and development. Regular movement supports physical fitness and contributes to cardiovascular, metabolic, musculoskeletal and psychological health.

For children and adolescents, movement is also connected with everyday life: playing, travelling to school, participating in physical education, spending time outdoors, enjoying recreation and connecting with friends.

But physical activity is not simply a matter of individual motivation. Whether young people can move safely and enjoyably is influenced by families, schools, neighbourhoods, transport, public spaces, economic circumstances, disability access, cultural expectations and many other social determinants of health.

LEARN

Ten Things to Explore

01

What counts as physical activity?

Discover how play, walking, cycling, sport, recreation, school activities and everyday movement all contribute to an active life.

02

What does “60 minutes a day” actually mean?

Understand WHO's recommendation and why it refers to an average across the week rather than a rigid daily performance test.

03

Moderate vs. vigorous activity

Learn how intensity differs and how young people can recognize different levels of physical effort.

04

Aerobic, muscle and bone strengthening

Understand the different forms of activity that contribute to physical development.

05

Movement, sitting and sedentary behaviour

Explore the importance of breaking up prolonged sedentary time and creating opportunities for movement.

06

Physical activity and learning

Explore the relationship between movement, concentration, cognition, school participation and educational wellbeing.

07

Physical activity and mental wellbeing

Consider how movement, recreation, social connection and time outdoors can contribute to wellbeing.

08

Why are some young people less active?

Examine the social, environmental, economic, cultural and structural barriers that shape opportunities for activity.

09

Creating active families, schools and communities

Discover how environments can make healthy movement easier, safer, more inclusive and more enjoyable.

10

From active young people to active societies

Connect individual experiences with population health, healthy environments, equity and the life-course perspective.

THINK GLOBALLY

Physical Activity and the Social Determinants of Health

The opportunity to be physically active is not distributed equally. Young people's experiences are shaped by the conditions in which they live, learn, travel, play and grow.

Local

Are there safe streets, parks, playgrounds, recreation spaces and accessible facilities?

National

Do policies, education systems, transportation, urban planning and public investment support active living?

Global

How do poverty, inequality, urbanization, disability, gender and environmental conditions influence opportunities for movement worldwide?

REFLECT

Ask Yourself

“What helps young people around me be physically active, and what makes it harder?”

Think beyond yourself. Consider your family, school, neighbourhood and community. Then ask whether everyone has the same opportunities to be active.

ACT

From Knowledge to Action

For Myself

Identify one enjoyable form of movement that you would like to include more often.

For Others

Invite a friend or family member to participate in an enjoyable and inclusive activity.

For My Community

Identify one barrier to physical activity around you and think about how it could be reduced.

For Global Health

Ask how healthier environments can create opportunities for active lives across populations.

QUIZ

Test Your Understanding

1. What is WHO's recommended average amount of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity for children and adolescents aged 5–17?

Answer: 60 minutes per day on average across the week.

2. Which statement best reflects a social-determinants perspective on physical activity?

Answer: Physical activity is influenced by individual, social, environmental and structural conditions.
