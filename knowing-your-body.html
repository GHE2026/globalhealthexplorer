01 · WHY IT MATTERS

Understanding your body is part of becoming health literate.

You do not need to become a doctor to understand your body. Basic knowledge of biology can help you understand health information, recognize reliable evidence, ask informed questions and make sense of how different factors influence health.

01

Understand

Learn what your body is made of and how its structures work together.

02

Question

Build enough scientific understanding to ask better health questions.

03

Connect

Discover how biology interacts with behaviour, environment and society.

02 · ANATOMY

Structure: What is the body made of?

Anatomy is the study of the structures of the body and how those structures are organized.

BODY

Cells

The basic living units of the human body. Different cells have specialized structures and functions.

Tissues

Groups of specialized cells organized to perform particular functions.

Organs

Structures made from different tissues that work together to perform important functions.

A central principle of biology: Structure and function are closely connected.
03 · PHYSIOLOGY

Function: How does the body work?

Physiology explores how cells, tissues, organs and systems perform their functions and interact with one another.

01

Communication

Nervous and endocrine systems help coordinate activities throughout the body.

02

Transport

Blood and circulation move oxygen, nutrients, hormones and other substances.

03

Exchange

The lungs, digestive system and other organs exchange materials with the environment.

04

Protection

Skin, immune defences and other systems help protect the body from threats.

04 · HOMEOSTASIS

Keeping the body's internal environment within a healthy range.

The body constantly monitors and adjusts many internal conditions. This dynamic regulation is called homeostasis.

HOMEOSTASIS Dynamic Balance Continuous adjustment rather than perfect stillness.
Temperature The body regulates heat production and heat loss.
Blood Glucose Hormonal systems help regulate glucose availability.
Water & Electrolytes The body regulates fluids and important dissolved substances.
Oxygen & Carbon Dioxide Respiratory and cardiovascular systems work together to maintain gas exchange.
Think like a scientist: The body is constantly responding to change. Healthy physiology is dynamic.
05 · BODY SYSTEMS

No body system works alone.

Each system has specialized functions, but healthy life depends on continuous interaction among systems.

01

Nervous System

Senses, processes information and coordinates rapid responses.

02

Cardiovascular System

Heart, blood and blood vessels transport materials throughout the body.

03

Respiratory System

Supports gas exchange and contributes to regulation of the body's internal environment.

04

Digestive System

Breaks down food and enables absorption of nutrients and water.

05

Musculoskeletal System

Provides support, movement and protection.

06

Immune & Lymphatic Systems

Help defend the body and maintain fluid and immune balance.

07

Endocrine System

Uses hormones to regulate processes such as growth, metabolism and reproduction.

08

Urinary System

Helps regulate fluids, electrolytes and removal of metabolic waste.

09

Reproductive System

Supports reproductive functions and contributes to human development.

10

Dermatology

Skin, hair and nails provide important protective, sensory and regulatory functions.

Systems thinking: The heart does not work alone. The lungs, blood, nervous system, kidneys, hormones and muscles all contribute to keeping the body alive.
06 · GENETICS

Genetics: What makes each person biologically unique?

Genetics explores how information in DNA is organized, expressed, inherited and sometimes changed. Genes influence many characteristics and aspects of health, but genes do not act alone.

Genetic Variation

People naturally differ in many biological characteristics. Variation is a fundamental feature of human populations.

Inheritance

Genetic information can be passed from biological parents to children through patterns of inheritance.

Genes & Environment

Health often reflects interactions among genetic, environmental, behavioural and social factors.

Remember: Genetic risk is not the same as certainty. Biology is important, but biology is not destiny.
GENETIC & INHERITED CONDITIONS

When genetic differences affect health.

Some health conditions are strongly influenced by genetic changes. Others result from complex interactions among many genes and environmental factors.

01

Single-Gene Conditions

Conditions associated with changes in a particular gene.

02

Chromosomal Conditions

Conditions associated with changes in the number or structure of chromosomes.

03

Multifactorial Conditions

Health outcomes influenced by multiple genes together with environmental and behavioural factors.

04

Genetic Counselling

Genetic information can be complex. Questions about personal or family genetic risk should be discussed with appropriately qualified health professionals.

07 · PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

What happens when normal function changes?

Pathophysiology examines how changes in normal biological processes contribute to disease, dysfunction and health outcomes.

NORMAL Structure & function
→
CHANGE Disruption or adaptation
→
CONSEQUENCE Altered biological function
→
HEALTH OUTCOME Possible symptoms, signs or disease

Inflammation

A coordinated biological response that can help the body respond to injury or infection but can also contribute to disease when dysregulated.

Infection

Microorganisms can interact with tissues and trigger immune responses that affect normal physiology.

Injury

Physical or other forms of injury can disrupt tissues and interfere with normal function.

Abnormal Cell Growth

Changes in cellular regulation can contribute to abnormal growth and disease processes.

Important health-literacy principle: A biological change, symptom or risk factor does not by itself establish a diagnosis.
08 · SYSTEMS THINKING

The body works as an interconnected network.

Understanding health often requires following a chain of interactions rather than looking at one organ in isolation.

01

Oxygen & Energy

Environment → Lungs → Blood → Tissues → Energy

Breathing and circulation work together to deliver oxygen where cells need it.

02

Regulation

Brain → Hormones → Organs → Cells → Response

Nervous and endocrine signalling helps coordinate many body functions.

03

Movement

Brain → Nerves → Muscles → Bones → Movement

Movement depends on coordinated activity across multiple systems.

09 · BIOLOGY + THE WORLD

Is health only inside the body?

Biology matters enormously—but people live in environments and societies that can influence exposure, opportunity, stress, nutrition, physical activity, safety and access to care.

Environment Air, water, climate and physical surroundings can affect health.
Nutrition Food availability and dietary patterns influence biological processes.
Education Knowledge and health literacy can influence health decisions and opportunities.
Housing & Safety Living conditions can influence exposure to hazards and opportunities for health.
Healthcare Access Access to appropriate services can influence prevention, diagnosis and treatment.
Equity Health opportunities are not distributed equally across populations.
The GHE perspective

The body is biological—but health is also social, environmental and global.

11 · EVIDENCE

Learn to distinguish evidence from assumption.

Understanding biology is also learning how scientific knowledge is developed, tested, revised and communicated.

ASK

Clearly identify what someone is saying about health or the human body.

CHECK

What is the evidence?

Look for trustworthy scientific and health sources.

THINK

How strong is it?

Consider whether the evidence actually supports the conclusion.

QUESTION

What remains uncertain?

Good science recognizes uncertainty and continues to investigate important questions.
