HEALTHY LIVING · SUBJECT 3

Sleep, Rest
& Recovery

Understanding why sleep matters for learning, wellbeing, healthy development and life across the life course.

GLOBAL EVIDENCE

How much sleep do adolescents need?

8–10 hours

A widely supported recommendation for teenagers

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends that teenagers aged 13–18 years should regularly sleep 8–10 hours per 24 hours to support optimal health and daytime alertness.

WHO's adolescent-health guidance emphasizes that adolescence is a critical period for physical, cognitive and psychosocial development and that young people need supportive environments in which to grow and thrive.

Sleep is more than a number.

Duration matters, but so do sleep quality, regularity, timing, the sleep environment and the conditions that make adequate sleep possible.

EXPLORE

Why is sleep important?

Sleep is an essential part of healthy development. During adolescence, young people experience rapid physical, cognitive and psychosocial changes, making adequate rest particularly important.

Good sleep supports attention, learning, memory, emotional regulation, physical recovery and everyday functioning. Inadequate sleep can interfere with these areas and can affect how young people experience school, relationships and daily life.

But sleep should not be framed simply as an individual's responsibility. School schedules, family circumstances, housing, noise, light, technology, work, caregiving, safety and socioeconomic conditions can all influence opportunities for healthy sleep.

THINK CRITICALLY

Sleep and the Social Determinants of Health

Not everyone has the same opportunity to obtain adequate, good-quality sleep. Sleep is influenced by conditions beyond individual behaviour.

Local

Is the home quiet, safe and comfortable? Is there crowding, noise, light or insecurity?

National

How do school schedules, labour policies, housing systems and social protection affect opportunities for healthy sleep?

Global

How do poverty, conflict, displacement, urbanization, inequality and environmental conditions shape sleep around the world?

QUIZ

Test Your Understanding

Ten questions to help you check what you have learned.

1. What amount of sleep is generally recommended for teenagers aged 13–18?

Answer: 8–10 hours.

2. Which statement best describes the importance of sleep?

Answer: Sleep supports learning, memory, wellbeing, physical recovery and daily functioning.

3. Why can adolescent sleep timing change during puberty?

Answer: Biological changes can influence circadian timing.

4. Which is an example of a social determinant of sleep?

Answer: All of the above.

5. Which statement best reflects a GHE perspective?

Answer: Healthy sleep involves both individual choices and supportive conditions.

6. Why might school schedules matter for adolescent sleep?

Answer: They can influence when students must wake up.

7. Which question demonstrates critical thinking about sleep?

Answer: “What social and environmental conditions affect people's opportunity to sleep?”

8. Which SDG is most directly connected with health and wellbeing, including healthy sleep?

Answer: SDG 3 — Good Health and Well-being.

9. Which action best reflects the GHE principle of creating healthier conditions for others?

Answer: Helping create environments that respect people's need for adequate rest.

10. What is the broader life-course message of this subject?

Answer: Healthy sleep during adolescence can be part of a broader foundation for wellbeing across life.
