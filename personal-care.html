01 WHY THIS MATTERS

Health is something we protect, promote and learn about throughout life.

01

Prevention

Some health problems can be prevented, reduced or detected early through healthy environments, vaccination, screening, hygiene, safety and evidence-informed care.

02

Self-care

WHO describes self-care as the ability of individuals, families and communities to promote and maintain health, prevent disease and cope with illness.

Self-care complements health services; it does not replace them.

03

Health literacy

Being able to find, understand, evaluate and use trustworthy health information helps people make informed decisions and participate in their own care.

GHE QUESTION

How can we become better informed about our health without assuming that every health problem can be solved by an individual alone?

02 PERSONAL HINTS

Small habits. Better information. Timely support.

Know your body

  • Learn what is normal for you.
  • Notice important changes rather than ignoring them.
  • Ask a trusted adult or health professional when you are unsure.

Build healthy routines

  • Practice good personal and hand hygiene.
  • Protect your skin from excessive ultraviolet radiation.
  • Keep preventive health activities up to date.

Be a critical health-information user

  • Ask where health information comes from.
  • Check whether the source is trustworthy.
  • Be cautious about dramatic health claims on social media.

Know when to ask for help

Asking for help is a health skill. A trusted adult, pharmacist, nurse, doctor or other qualified professional can help when a question goes beyond what you can safely manage yourself.

04 EXPLORE

What does personal preventive health include?

Personal care is much more than cleanliness. It includes the knowledge, habits, preventive services and support systems that help people stay healthy and respond appropriately when health problems arise.

01

Personal hygiene

Everyday practices that help maintain health and reduce the spread of infection.

02

03

Understanding UV exposure, skin health and sensible protection.

04

05

06

07

Preventive health

Vaccination, preventive visits, screening and evidence-informed care.

08

05 LEARN

Twelve skills for a healthier life

01

Personal hygiene

Hygiene is a health skill, not a measure of personal worth. Hand hygiene is especially important because contaminated hands can contribute to the spread of infectious diseases.

WHO's 2025 community hand-hygiene guidance emphasizes not only information, but also access to appropriate materials and a supportive environment. CDC likewise identifies handwashing as an effective way to reduce the spread of germs.

02

Screening

Screening is not simply “getting every test.” A screening test is useful when there is evidence that testing a particular population can identify disease or risk early enough to improve outcomes.

Think critically: More testing is not automatically better. Screening should be evidence-informed and appropriate to the person and population.
03

Skin and sun protection

Ultraviolet radiation can damage skin. Health Canada recommends practical sun protection such as shade, protective clothing, sunglasses and sunscreen when appropriate.

UV protection is about health and prevention, not appearance. Tanning equipment can expose skin to harmful UV radiation and should be avoided.

04

Personal safety

Health includes the conditions around us. Safe homes, schools, roads, recreational spaces and communities reduce preventable injuries.

Personal safety therefore involves both individual awareness and safer systems.

05

Injury prevention

Injury prevention means identifying hazards, understanding risk and improving the environment before harm occurs.

This is a public-health approach: prevention should not depend entirely on expecting individuals to behave perfectly.

06

Knowing your body

Bodies change with age, development, activity, stress and many other factors. Learning what is typical for you can help you recognize changes that deserve attention.

A change does not automatically mean disease. If something concerns you, ask a trusted adult or qualified health professional.

07

Preventive health

Preventive health can include recommended immunizations, preventive visits, counselling, screening when appropriate and actions that reduce known health risks.

CDC guidance emphasizes regular preventive health care for adolescents, including recommended immunizations, screening and counselling according to individual circumstances.

08

First aid

First aid is the immediate assistance given when someone is injured or becomes suddenly unwell while appropriate professional help is being arranged.

A key health-literacy skill is knowing the limits of first aid. Serious or uncertain situations require appropriate emergency or professional assistance.

09

When should you seek help?

Seeking help early can be an important part of prevention and safety. New, persistent, worsening or concerning symptoms deserve appropriate attention rather than being dismissed automatically.

GHE principle: Asking for help is not weakness. It is health literacy.
10

Health literacy

WHO defines health literacy as involving the ability to access, understand, appraise and use information and services in ways that promote and maintain health and well-being.

Health literacy also means asking good questions, recognizing uncertainty and understanding when professional advice is needed.

11

Understanding medicines and health advice

Medicines can be helpful when used appropriately, but health decisions should be based on reliable information and professional advice when needed.

Health Canada recommends asking healthcare professionals why a medicine is being used, how it should work, possible side effects and possible interactions.

Never guess: If you do not understand a medicine, ask a pharmacist, doctor or another qualified healthcare professional.
12

Navigating health services

Health literacy includes knowing where to go for help and how to communicate with health professionals.

  • Prepare your questions.
  • Describe what has changed or concerns you.
  • Ask what the diagnosis or concern means.
  • Ask what the options are.
  • Ask what follow-up is needed.
06 HEALTHY CHOICES

Prevention begins with informed choices — and supportive environments.

Keep basic hygiene practical

Make clean hands and personal hygiene normal parts of everyday life.

Protect your skin

Pay attention to UV exposure and use sensible sun protection.

Stay up to date

Learn which preventive services and vaccinations are recommended for you.

Ask questions

Good healthcare involves communication, not passive acceptance of information.

Check the source

Prefer WHO, CDC, Health Canada, public-health agencies and qualified professionals over unsupported online claims.

Ask for help

Know when a health concern needs support beyond self-care.

07 THINK

Prevention is not the same as perfection.

Can everyone make the same choices?

No. Income, housing, education, language, disability, transportation, geography, discrimination and access to services can all influence health opportunities.

Is health information equally accessible?

Not necessarily. Information may be difficult to understand, unavailable in someone's language or inaccessible because of digital, educational or economic barriers.

Is more information always better?

Not if it is inaccurate, confusing or contradictory. Health literacy requires appraisal, not simply collecting information.

Who is responsible for prevention?

Individuals matter, but so do families, schools, healthcare systems, communities, governments and the wider environment.

09 SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Health choices happen inside social conditions.

WHO emphasizes that health literacy is shaped not only by personal knowledge but also by the organizational structures and resources that determine whether people can access and use trustworthy information and services.

Income

Can influence access to food, transportation, medicines, dental care and other services.

Education

Helps shape health knowledge, communication and the ability to evaluate information.

Language

Health information should be understandable and culturally and linguistically appropriate.

Digital access

Internet access and digital skills increasingly influence access to health information and services.

Healthcare access

Availability, affordability and quality of care influence whether prevention is possible.

Discrimination

Stigma and discrimination can create barriers to information, prevention and care.

Housing & environment

Safe housing, clean environments and supportive neighbourhoods influence health.

Age & life stage

Health needs and appropriate preventive services change throughout the life course.

EQUITY QUESTION

If trustworthy health information exists but some people cannot access, understand or use it, has the health system done enough?

10 PREVENTION IN ACTION

Prevention works at several levels.

01

Promote health

Create conditions that make healthy choices easier: safe environments, education, nutritious food, physical activity, social connection and access to care.

02

Prevent disease

Reduce known risks through vaccination, hygiene, safety, environmental protection and evidence-informed prevention.

03

Detect early

Appropriate screening and timely assessment can identify some health problems earlier.

04

Respond appropriately

When illness or injury occurs, timely, appropriate professional care can reduce complications and support recovery.

GHE PRINCIPLE

Prevention is strongest when personal action and supportive systems work together.

11 HEALTH LITERACY

Become a better health-information detective.

01

FIND

Where did this information come from? Can I identify the organization or author?

02

UNDERSTAND

Do I actually understand the terminology, numbers and recommendations?

03

APPRAISE

Is the evidence credible? What might be missing? Is the claim exaggerated?

04

USE

What action is appropriate, and when should I ask a qualified professional?

A useful source hierarchy

For important health questions, start with authoritative public-health and healthcare sources such as WHO, national public-health agencies, Health Canada, CDC, qualified healthcare professionals and evidence-based clinical guidance.

A search result, influencer post or anonymous social-media claim is not automatically evidence.

13 GLOBAL HEALTH & EVIDENCE

Personal health is a global-health subject.

WHO

Self-care

WHO's 2026 self-care guidance describes self-care as an important component of health promotion and disease prevention while emphasizing an enabling environment and connection with health systems.

WHO

Health literacy

WHO identifies health literacy as a determinant of health and emphasizes that people and organizations share responsibility for making health information understandable and actionable.

WHO · UNICEF

Hand hygiene

The 2025 WHO/UNICEF community hand-hygiene guidelines emphasize access to materials, information and a supportive environment.

CDC

Adolescent preventive care

CDC identifies regular preventive health care as important for adolescents, including recommended immunizations, screening and counselling according to individual needs.

HEALTH CANADA

Sun safety

Health Canada provides guidance on UV protection, including shade, protective clothing, sunglasses and appropriate sunscreen use.

HEALTH CANADA

Medication safety

Health Canada advises people to understand their medicines, ask healthcare professionals questions, store medicines safely and dispose of unused medicines appropriately.

ONE STEP YOU CAN TAKE

Choose one health question you have and find the answer from a trustworthy source.

Then ask yourself: What evidence supports the answer? What remains uncertain? And when would I need professional advice?

15 TEST YOURSELF · 12 MCQs

Test your health-literacy thinking.

These questions move from basic knowledge to prevention, equity, evidence and health-system thinking. Open each question to check your answer.

1. Which statement best describes self-care?

Answer: B. Self-care includes actions that help people, families and communities promote and maintain health, prevent disease and cope with illness.

2. What is health literacy?

Answer: C. Health literacy includes the ability to access, understand, appraise and use health information and services appropriately.

3. Which is an example of preventive health?

Answer: A. Recommended vaccination, appropriate screening and preventive health visits are examples of preventive care.

4. Why is hand hygiene important?

Answer: B. Good hand hygiene can reduce the spread of infectious diseases, including some diarrhoeal and respiratory infections.

5. Which statement about screening is most accurate?

Answer: D. Screening should be evidence-informed and appropriate to the population and health question; more testing is not automatically better.

6. Which organization provides health information and sun-safety guidance in Canada?

Answer: C. Health Canada is a major authoritative Canadian source for public-health information, including sun-safety guidance.

7. Which is the best response when you do not understand a medicine or health recommendation?

Answer: B. Ask a pharmacist, doctor or another qualified healthcare professional rather than guessing.

8. Why is health literacy also an equity issue?

Answer: A. People do not have equal access to education, understandable information, digital resources or healthcare services, so health-literacy opportunities can differ.

9. Which statement best reflects the GHE approach to prevention?

Answer: D. Prevention combines informed individual action with supportive families, communities, health systems and public policy.

10. Which question is most useful when evaluating an online health claim?

Answer: C. Ask who produced the information, what evidence supports it and whether the claim is consistent with trustworthy health authorities and research.

11. Why can personal health not be separated completely from health systems?

Answer: B. Prevention and care depend partly on access to information, professionals, medicines, vaccines, screening and other health services.

12. Which statement best represents global health leadership?

Answer: A. A global health leader combines evidence, health literacy, equity, prevention and action across personal, community, national and international levels.
