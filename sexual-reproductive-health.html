OUR CORE PRINCIPLE

Health is personal — and social.

“I can make healthy choices for myself, and I can help create healthier conditions for others.”

Sexual and reproductive health is shaped not only by individual knowledge and decisions, but also by education, family and peer relationships, gender norms, poverty, discrimination, safety, health services, policies and the wider social environment.

EXPLORE

Why is this important?

Adolescence is a period of rapid physical, cognitive and psychosocial development. Accurate, age-appropriate information about the body, relationships, consent, prevention, reproductive health and health services can help young people make informed decisions and know where to seek trustworthy support.

Good information is not the same as telling someone what decision to make.

GHE helps young people understand evidence, ask questions, evaluate information and identify appropriate sources of support.

GHE SPOTLIGHT • CANCER PREVENTION

HPV vaccination: prevention starts early

Human papillomavirus (HPV) is extremely common. Persistent infection with high-risk HPV types causes approximately 99% of cervical cancers and is also associated with several other cancers.

HPV vaccination can prevent the infection that causes cervical cancer.

It is one of the most important cancer-prevention interventions available to adolescents.

Remember: HPV vaccination does not replace cervical cancer screening. Vaccination and screening are complementary prevention strategies.

From personal health to health equity

A young person may have the knowledge to make a healthy decision but still face barriers to vaccination, screening, information, confidential services or safe environments.

That makes sexual and reproductive health a health-equity and health-systems issue.

What can a Global Health Explorer do?

GHE does not say: “Your health is entirely your responsibility.”

“I can make healthy choices for myself, and I can help create healthier conditions for others.”
  • Check health claims against reliable sources.
  • Learn about recommended adolescent vaccinations.
  • Support respectful and safe relationships.
  • Challenge health misinformation.
  • Reduce stigma around seeking healthcare.
  • Encourage friends to use trustworthy health information.
  • Learn how health systems can improve adolescent access.
  • Become an advocate for equitable health education.
