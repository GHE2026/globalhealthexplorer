Poverty, Income
& Inequality

Does having more income always mean better health?

Poverty is more than a number in a bank account. Income, education, housing, nutrition, employment, social protection, access to services and opportunities can interact to shape people's lives — and their health.

01 • START HERE

Poverty, Income and Inequality Are Not the Same Thing

These concepts are connected, but they answer different questions. Good global-health analysis starts by knowing which question a measure is designed to answer.

P

Poverty

Poverty describes a lack of resources or capabilities needed to achieve an acceptable standard of living. It can be measured using monetary or multidimensional approaches.

$

Income

Income is the flow of resources received over a period of time. It can come from employment, transfers, pensions, investments and other sources.

↕

Inequality

Inequality describes how resources, opportunities, outcomes or other measurable characteristics differ across people or groups.

◎

Opportunity

Opportunities include access to education, decent work, health care, safe housing, social protection and the ability to participate in society.

Think like a data scientist: Before comparing two places, ask: What exactly is being measured?
02 • MEASURE

How Do We Measure Poverty?

Different measures reveal different dimensions of people's lives. A single poverty number cannot capture every form of deprivation.

01

Monetary poverty

Measures whether people's resources fall below a specified poverty threshold.

What can people afford?
02

Multidimensional poverty

Looks at multiple simultaneous deprivations in areas such as health, education and living standards.

What are people deprived of?
03

Income distribution

Examines how income or consumption is distributed across the population.

How are resources distributed?
04

Health inequality

Examines measurable differences in health between population groups.

Who experiences different outcomes?
WORLD BANK • GLOBAL SNAPSHOT

Poverty Reduction Has Slowed

The World Bank's 2024 assessment highlights a major challenge: global poverty reduction has slowed sharply, while inequality and vulnerability continue to affect shared prosperity.

~700 million people were living in extreme poverty in the World Bank's 2024 assessment.
44% of the global population was estimated to live below the $6.85/day upper-middle-income poverty standard.
1 in 5 people were identified as being at risk of an extreme weather event over their lifetime.

World Bank poverty figures use specific international poverty lines and purchasing-power-parity concepts. They should not be interpreted as equivalent to a country's national poverty line.

GHE insight

A person can have income above a poverty threshold and still experience serious deprivation in another dimension. Conversely, income alone does not tell us how resources are distributed within a household.

03 • THINK SYSTEMS

Poverty and Health Can Reinforce Each Other

Poverty is not simply an outcome. It can also shape exposure to risks and access to protective resources, while poor health can reduce the ability to work, learn and maintain financial security.

1 Limited resources Income, assets or social protection
↓
2 Living conditions Housing, food, water, environment
↓
3 Health risks Exposure, stress, illness
↓
4 Reduced opportunity Work, learning and participation
↺
Important: This is not a simple one-way chain. The relationships are influenced by policies, institutions, social protection, education, health systems, labour markets and community resources.

Income & employment

Stable employment and adequate income can provide access to food, housing, transportation, education and health-protective resources.

02

Economic insecurity can affect the quality, safety, stability and location of housing.

03

CONNECT THE WORLD

Poverty and Climate Risk Can Overlap

The 2025 UNDP Multidimensional Poverty Index adds another important dimension: environmental hazards. Poverty can increase vulnerability to shocks, while climate hazards can damage livelihoods and deepen deprivation.

887 million multidimensionally poor people were living in regions exposed to at least one of four climate hazards in the 2025 MPI analysis.
309 million poor people were living in regions exposed to three or four of those hazards.
740 million multidimensionally poor people lived in middle-income countries in the 2025 MPI.
GHE Systems Question

If a community has low income, poor housing and limited access to services, what might happen when a climate shock, food-price increase or infectious-disease outbreak occurs?

Look for overlapping vulnerabilities rather than studying each problem in isolation.

GHE DATA DETECTIVE

Six Questions Before You Compare Two Places

01

Same definition?

Are both places using the same poverty or inequality definition?

02

Same year?

Are the observations from comparable periods?

03

Same denominator?

Are rates, percentages or counts based on comparable populations?

04

Average or subgroup?

Could national averages hide differences between groups?

05

Income or deprivation?

Is the indicator measuring resources, outcomes, access or multiple deprivations?

06

Association or cause?

Does the evidence demonstrate a causal relationship, or only a statistical association?

GHE REFLECTION

Every difference matters. Explore it.

A country's average income can tell you something important — but never everything. Ask who has access to resources, who faces barriers, who experiences multiple disadvantages and whether opportunities are changing over time.
