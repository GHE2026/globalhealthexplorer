UNDERSTANDING HEALTH THREATS
01 · UNDERSTAND HEALTH EVENTS

How does the world recognize a health threat?

Public-health systems use surveillance, assessment, communication and coordinated response to understand changing health risks.

SURVEILLANCE Collect and watch health data for signals.
ASSESSMENT Understand what a signal may mean.
COMMUNICATION Share clear, timely and honest information.
COORDINATION Governments and partners work together.
RESPONSE Take appropriate public-health action.
HEALTH EVENT VOCABULARY

Different words describe different situations.

These are concepts to understand—not instructions for conducting professional outbreak investigations.

Outbreak
More cases of a disease than expected in a particular place or group.
Surge
A sudden increase in cases, demand or another health-related measure.
Epidemic
Cases occurring in a community or region above what is normally expected.
Pandemic
An epidemic that spreads across countries or continents and affects many people.
PHEIC
A Public Health Emergency of International Concern under the International Health Regulations.
Pandemic Emergency
A high-level global alert category introduced through the amended International Health Regulations for qualifying pandemic risks.
GLOBAL HEALTH GOVERNANCE

Who helps countries work together?

International cooperation complements national and local public-health responsibilities.

WHO + IHR

International Health Regulations

The International Health Regulations provide a global framework for countries to prevent, prepare for, detect, assess and respond to public-health risks that may cross borders.

The amended IHR entered into force in 2025 and introduced a new pandemic emergency alert level.

PANDEMIC AGREEMENT · 2025

Cooperation for future pandemics

The WHO Pandemic Agreement was adopted by the World Health Assembly in 2025 to strengthen pandemic prevention, preparedness and response and improve equity.

Its implementation is part of an ongoing global process.

Important: WHO provides global coordination, technical guidance and information. Governments and their public-health authorities make and implement decisions within their own jurisdictions.
02 · DETECT, UNDERSTAND & COMMUNICATE

Information is part of the response.

During a health threat, people need information that is timely, understandable, honest about uncertainty and connected to the communities affected.

RCCE—risk communication and community engagement—is about communication with people, not simply communication at people.

Listening and two-way engagement help build trust, understand concerns and make public-health communication more useful.

VACCINES + POINTS OF ENTRY

Prevention and preparedness are connected.

Vaccines and Points of Entry illustrate how individual, community, national and international systems can work together.

VACCINES

A powerful prevention tool

Vaccines train the immune system to recognize specific infections. Examples include vaccines against measles, polio, influenza, COVID-19, HPV, hepatitis B and yellow fever.

Different vaccines have different uses, schedules and levels of protection. Current recommendations should always come from trusted public-health authorities.

POINTS OF ENTRY · POEs

Where health security meets travel

Airports, seaports and ground crossings can help detect potential risks, provide health information to travellers, coordinate with public-health authorities and support measures appropriate to the situation.

POEs are part of a broader public-health system—not simply places to “stop sick people.”

POEs can be particularly important for certain internationally connected health risks and diseases.
