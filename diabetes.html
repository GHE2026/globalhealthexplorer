WHY IS THIS IMPORTANT?

Diabetes is a global health challenge

Diabetes is a chronic disease that affects how the body regulates glucose — the main sugar circulating in the blood.

When diabetes is not adequately controlled, persistently high blood glucose can damage blood vessels, nerves, eyes and kidneys, and can increase the risk of cardiovascular disease.

But diabetes is not simply a story about individual behaviour. The opportunities people have to eat nutritious food, be physically active, obtain medicines and access health care are strongly influenced by their social, economic and physical environments.

That makes diabetes an excellent subject for learning global health, equity, systems thinking and evidence-based leadership.

🩸
830 million
people living with diabetes in 2022
WHO estimates that the number increased from about 200 million in 1990 to 830 million in 2022.

01 · EXPLORE

What exactly is diabetes?

🩸

Blood glucose

Glucose is an important source of energy for the body. Its level in the blood is regulated through several biological processes, including the action of insulin.

🔑

Insulin

Insulin is a hormone produced by the pancreas that helps glucose move from the bloodstream into cells where it can be used for energy.

1️⃣

Type 1 diabetes

An autoimmune condition in which the body's immune system damages the insulin-producing cells of the pancreas. People with type 1 diabetes require insulin.

2️⃣

Type 2 diabetes

The most common form of diabetes. It develops through a combination of insulin resistance and insufficient insulin production, influenced by biological, environmental and social factors.

🤰

Gestational diabetes

Diabetes that is diagnosed during pregnancy. It can affect the health of the pregnant person and baby and is associated with a higher future risk of type 2 diabetes.

⚠️

Hyperglycaemia

A condition in which blood glucose is higher than the normal range. Persistent hyperglycaemia can damage blood vessels and other organs over time.

02 · LEARN

How does diabetes affect the body?

Think of glucose regulation as a system rather than a single organ.

1

Food & glucose

Carbohydrates in food can be broken down into glucose, which enters the bloodstream.

→
2

Insulin & regulation

Insulin helps regulate glucose by enabling cells to take up and use glucose.

→
3

High blood glucose

When glucose regulation is impaired, blood glucose can remain too high.

→
4

Long-term effects

Persistent high blood glucose can damage blood vessels, nerves, kidneys and eyes and increase cardiovascular risk.

Important: Diabetes is not one disease with one cause. Different types have different biological mechanisms, and a person's circumstances also influence prevention, diagnosis, treatment and outcomes.
UNDERSTANDING DIFFERENT TYPES

Diabetes does not have one single story

TYPE 1

Autoimmune diabetes

The immune system attacks insulin-producing cells in the pancreas.

  • Usually requires insulin for survival.
  • It is not caused simply by eating sugar.
  • It can develop in children, adolescents and adults.
TYPE 2

The most common form

The body becomes less responsive to insulin and, over time, may not produce enough insulin to maintain normal glucose levels.

  • Risk is influenced by multiple factors.
  • Family history and biology can matter.
  • Social and physical environments also influence risk.
PREGNANCY

Gestational diabetes

Diabetes diagnosed during pregnancy can affect both pregnancy outcomes and future health.

  • Requires appropriate health-care support.
  • Is not a reason to blame the pregnant person.
  • Can be connected to later risk of type 2 diabetes.

05 · SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Diabetes is shaped by the conditions of daily life

WHO describes social determinants as the conditions in which people are born, grow, live, work and age, together with access to power, money and resources.

💰 Income & affordability

Household resources can influence access to nutritious food, safe housing, transportation, recreation, medicines and health services.

🥗 Food environments

What is affordable, available, convenient and heavily marketed can influence dietary patterns across communities.

🏙️ Built environment

Safe streets, parks, transportation and neighbourhood design can influence opportunities for physical activity.

🎓 Education

Education and health literacy can influence people's ability to understand health information and navigate health systems.

🏥 Health-system access

Diagnosis, medicines, insulin, monitoring, follow-up and treatment of complications depend on accessible and effective health systems.

📢 Commercial determinants

Marketing, product design, pricing, distribution and other commercial practices can shape the environments in which people make health-related decisions.

🌍 Geography & inequality

Where people live can influence exposure to risk, access to services and the resources available to prevent and manage diabetes.

🤝 Social support

Families, peers, schools and communities can influence health knowledge, emotional support and the ability to manage chronic conditions.

🌡️ Climate & changing environments

Climate change can affect food systems, livelihoods, physical activity opportunities and the broader determinants of health.

GHE principle: People make choices — but choices are made within environments. Creating healthier environments is therefore a shared responsibility.

07 · GLOBAL LEADERSHIP & EVIDENCE

Who is working on diabetes?

Global health is a network of evidence, policy, financing, regulation, implementation and community action.

WHO

World Health Organization

Develops global guidance, supports countries, monitors diabetes and leads international initiatives including the Global Diabetes Compact.

IDF

International Diabetes Federation

Represents diabetes organizations worldwide and produces the Diabetes Atlas, a major source of global diabetes estimates.

UN

United Nations

Places NCD prevention and health equity within the Sustainable Development Goals and wider global development agenda.

IHME

Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation

Produces global comparative estimates of diseases, risk factors, disability and mortality.

NATIONAL

National & local authorities

Ministries of health, municipalities, schools and public-health agencies influence prevention, regulation, primary care and access to treatment.

SCIENCE

Research & clinical communities

Researchers and professional organizations generate evidence about diabetes biology, prevention, treatment, health systems and implementation.

GLOBAL DATA SNAPSHOT

What does the evidence tell us?

Different sources measure different populations and use different methods. Good data literacy means asking what each number actually represents.

WHO 830 million

people living with diabetes in 2022, using WHO's global estimate.

IDF · 2025 589 million

adults aged 20–79 living with diabetes in 2024 according to the IDF Diabetes Atlas.

IDF · PROJECTION 853 million

adults aged 20–79 projected to be living with diabetes in 2050.

IDF · EQUITY 81%

of adults with diabetes are estimated to live in low- and middle-income countries.

🧠 Data-literacy challenge

Why are the WHO and IDF numbers different? Before deciding that one number is “wrong,” investigate the population covered, age range, year and methodology.
