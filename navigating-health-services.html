01 · WHY THIS MATTERS

Health services are a journey — not just a place.

Health care can involve many different people, organizations and levels of service. You might begin by looking for reliable information, speak with a primary-care professional, receive a referral, visit a specialist, use community services, or need urgent or emergency care.

Understanding how these pieces fit together can make it easier to seek appropriate help, communicate clearly, prepare for appointments and follow up on the next steps.

WHO emphasizes primary health care as a foundation for health systems because it brings health services closer to communities and supports prevention, treatment, rehabilitation and continuing care.

Think like a Global Health Explorer:

Don't ask only: "Where is the hospital?"

Ask: "What kind of help do I need, and what is the appropriate place to start?"

What is a health system?

A health system is much bigger than hospitals and doctors. It includes people, organizations, policies, financing, information, medicines, public-health programs and community services that work together to protect and improve health.

05 · PREPARE

Be an active participant in your care.

BEFORE

Prepare

  • Write down the main reason for your visit.
  • Note important questions you want to ask.
  • Bring relevant information requested by the service.
  • Consider asking a trusted adult to accompany or support you when appropriate.
DURING

Participate

  • Explain your concern clearly.
  • Ask if you do not understand a term.
  • Ask what the next step is.
  • Ask where you can find reliable information afterward.
AFTER

Follow Up

  • Remember the agreed next step.
  • Keep information that the service tells you to keep.
  • Know who to contact if you have questions about the plan.
  • Seek appropriate help if your situation changes or becomes urgent.
06 · ASK GOOD QUESTIONS

Questions are a health skill.

You do not need to know every medical term to participate in your health care. Asking clear questions can help turn complicated information into understandable next steps.

UNDERSTAND

"What does this mean?"

Ask for unfamiliar words or concepts to be explained in plain language.

PURPOSE

"Why is this being recommended?"

Understanding the purpose can help you make informed decisions.

OPTIONS

"What are the options?"

When appropriate, ask whether there are different reasonable approaches.

NEXT STEP

"What happens next?"

Clarify follow-up, timing and who you should contact if you have further questions.

URGENCY

"When should I seek further help?"

Ask what changes or warning signs should prompt you to seek additional care.

INFORMATION

"Where can I learn more?"

Ask for trustworthy sources rather than relying on random online posts.

07 · REFERRALS & COORDINATION

Health care works better when the pieces connect.

A referral can connect a person with another professional or service when additional expertise or support is needed.

In a well-coordinated system, information and responsibilities are communicated across services as appropriate, while respecting privacy and consent requirements.

Coordination matters especially when people use multiple services or need continuing care.

REFERRAL Connecting someone to another service or professional.
COORDINATION Helping different parts of care work together.
CONTINUITY Supporting care over time rather than treating every encounter as completely separate.
FOLLOW-UP Making sure the next step is understood and does not simply disappear.
08 · SOCIAL DETERMINANTS & EQUITY

Access is not the same as opportunity.

Having a health service somewhere in a country does not automatically mean that every person can use it easily.

Transportation, geography, income, housing, disability access, language, digital connectivity, discrimination, education, trust and cultural safety can all influence people's ability to obtain appropriate care.

A Global Health Explorer therefore asks not only whether a service exists, but whether people can reach it, afford it, understand it and use it safely and respectfully.

09 · CANADA → WORLD

Health systems differ — the principles connect us.

CANADA

Learn your local pathway

Health services and access pathways vary by province, territory, community and service. Learning how local services work is part of health literacy.

A young person should know which trusted adults, school resources and appropriate health professionals can help them understand local options.

GLOBAL HEALTH

Ask who is being left behind

Around the world, health systems differ in organization, resources, financing and access. Yet the goal of equitable, quality, people-centred care is universal.

Global Health Explorers compare systems while remembering that countries cannot simply copy one another: solutions must fit local needs, resources, cultures and communities.

10 · UNIVERSAL HEALTH COVERAGE

Health for all means more than having a clinic.

UHC

Universal Health Coverage

WHO defines universal health coverage as ensuring that all people can access the full range of quality health services they need, when and where they need them, without financial hardship.

11 · DIGITAL HEALTH

Digital tools can connect people — but they can also create new barriers.

OPPORTUNITY

Digital access

Online information, telehealth and digital services can make some forms of care and information easier to reach.

CHALLENGE

Digital exclusion

Not everyone has the same access to devices, connectivity, digital skills, accessible interfaces or trustworthy information.

RESPONSIBILITY

Digital health literacy

Learn to evaluate online information, recognize unreliable claims, protect privacy and know when an online source cannot replace professional care.

12 · SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH LENS

Ask what makes access easier or harder.

Health-service navigation is strongly connected to the social determinants of health.

Imagine two people with the same health concern. One may have transportation, stable housing, internet access, supportive adults and flexible time. The other may face several barriers before even reaching a service.

Equity means recognizing these differences and designing systems that reduce avoidable barriers.

14 · GHE EXPLORER PROJECT

Map the health services around you.

With a teacher, school staff member or qualified health professional supervising the project, explore how easily young people can understand and navigate local health services.

01 QUESTION What health services do young people know about?
02 MAP Identify trusted local and community resources.
03 CHECK Examine accessibility, clarity and barriers.
04 LISTEN Collect voluntary, anonymous feedback.
05 ANALYZE Look for patterns without identifying individuals.
06 ACT Suggest one practical improvement.

Keep the project safe and educational

  • Participation should be voluntary.
  • Follow school policies and obtain any required parent/guardian permission or student assent.
  • Do not collect names, addresses, diagnoses, medication information or other sensitive personal health information.
  • Report findings only in aggregated form.
  • Use validated questionnaires only with appropriate supervision and permission/licensing.
  • Treat the activity as a learning or quality-improvement exercise unless formal research approval has been obtained.
15 · EXERCISES

Practice navigating health services

EXERCISE 01

Draw Your Health-System Map

Starting with "ME", draw a pathway showing how someone might move from a health question to appropriate information, primary care, specialist care or another service.

Challenge: Where could someone become confused or lost?
EXERCISE 02

Health-Service Detective

With a teacher or trusted adult, choose one local health service. Find out what it does, who it serves, how people access it and where trustworthy information about it can be found.

Challenge: Can another student understand the pathway using only your explanation?
EXERCISE 03

Barrier Detective

Imagine that three students need the same service but have different transportation, language, financial, disability or digital-access situations.

Challenge: What could the system change to make access fairer?
16 · GLOBAL LEADERSHIP & EVIDENCE

Great health systems are designed with people, not just for people.

WHO's people-centred approach emphasizes engaging and empowering people and communities, strengthening governance and accountability, coordinating services and creating environments that support quality care.

This means that health-system navigation is not only an individual skill. It is also a system responsibility.

18 · GHE KNOWLEDGE CHECK

Test your health-system navigation skills

Choose the best answer. The goal is not to memorize a particular country's health system, but to develop the reasoning skills needed to navigate health services responsibly.

01

What is one important role of primary care?

Answer

B. Primary care can provide first-contact, continuing and coordinated care.

02

What does "coordination of care" mean?

Answer

C. Coordination helps different services and professionals work together.

03

Which question is especially useful at the end of an appointment?

Answer

A. Clarifying the next step improves understanding and follow-up.

04

Which is a social determinant that can affect access to care?

Answer

D. All can influence people's ability to obtain and use health services.

05

What is universal health coverage trying to achieve?

Answer

B. UHC includes access to needed quality health services without financial hardship.

06

Why might a referral be needed?

Answer

A. Referrals can connect people to additional expertise or services.

07

Which statement best describes people-centred care?

Answer

B. People-centred care puts people, families and communities at the centre.

08

What should you do if you do not understand a health term?

Answer

B. Asking questions is an important health-literacy skill.

09

Which statement about digital health is most accurate?

Answer

C. Digital health can create opportunities while also producing digital-access and literacy barriers.

10

What is the best approach when mapping local health services for a school project?

Answer

C. A youth learning project should minimize data collection and protect privacy.

11

Why is equity important in health-service navigation?

B. Different social and structural conditions can create unequal opportunities to obtain care.

19 · REFLECT

What does a navigable health system look like?

Imagine you are new to a community and do not know how its health system works.

Could you find trustworthy information? Would you know where to start? Could you communicate in a language and format you understand? Could you afford or physically reach the service? Would you know what happens next?

Reflect: What is one change that could make your local health system easier for young people to navigate?
