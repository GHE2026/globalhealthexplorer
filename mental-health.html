EXPLORE

Mental health is part of being human.

We all have mental health. It can change throughout life and is influenced by our experiences, relationships, environments and opportunities.

01

Wellbeing

Mental wellbeing includes our ability to learn, develop relationships, cope with everyday challenges and participate meaningfully in life.

02

Mental Health Conditions

Mental health conditions can affect thoughts, emotions, behaviour and daily functioning. They are health conditions, not personal failures.

03

Everyone Matters

People experience mental health differently. Understanding diversity, reducing stigma and creating supportive environments benefit everyone.

PROTECT

What helps protect mental health?

Mental health is influenced by many factors. Protective factors can strengthen wellbeing and resilience, although no single factor can guarantee a particular outcome.

Healthy sleep Regular, sufficient sleep supports physical and emotional wellbeing.
Physical activity Movement can support both physical and mental wellbeing.
Social connection Supportive relationships can provide belonging, encouragement and practical support.
Skills and coping Problem-solving, emotional skills and healthy coping strategies can strengthen resilience.
Supportive environments Safe families, schools and communities can promote mental wellbeing.
RESILIENCE

Resilience is not “never struggling.”

Resilience is the capacity to adapt, recover and continue developing when facing challenges. It can be supported by relationships, skills, resources and safe environments.

THINK

Mental Health Risk Factors

Risk is not destiny. Mental health outcomes emerge from the interaction of many individual, social, environmental and structural factors.

SELF

Individual

Emotional skills, biology, health behaviours and previous experiences can influence vulnerability.

FAMILY & PEERS

Relationships

Conflict, bullying, isolation and lack of support can increase vulnerability, while caring relationships can provide protection.

COMMUNITY

Environment

Schools, neighbourhoods, safety, inclusion and access to community resources all matter.

COUNTRY

Systems

Poverty, inequality, education, social protection and availability of health services influence opportunities for mental wellbeing.

WORLD

Global Forces

Conflict, displacement, humanitarian emergencies, climate change, disease outbreaks and global inequalities can affect mental health.

BREAK THE STIGMA

A diagnosis does not define a person.

Stigma can turn a health challenge into isolation. It can make people afraid to talk, seek support or participate fully in school, work and community life.

We can challenge stigma by listening respectfully, learning from evidence, avoiding stereotypes and treating people with dignity.

Learn Replace myths with evidence.
Listen Respect people's experiences.
Include Challenge exclusion and discrimination.
Support Encourage appropriate help-seeking.
PREVENT & SUPPORT

Prevention and Early Support

Promoting mental health is not only about treating conditions. It is also about creating environments where people can thrive.

01

Promote wellbeing

Encourage healthy routines, supportive relationships, meaningful activities and opportunities to participate.

02

Notice changes

Persistent changes in mood, behaviour, relationships, learning or daily functioning may be reasons to talk with a trusted adult or qualified professional.

03

Seek support

Asking for help is a strength. Support may come from trusted adults, school resources, community services or health professionals.

04

Keep people connected

Inclusion, belonging and respectful relationships can be important parts of mental health promotion.

HELP-SEEKING

You do not have to figure everything out alone.

If something is affecting your wellbeing or daily life, consider talking with a trusted adult, teacher, school counsellor, family member, healthcare professional or another appropriate support person.

A simple GHE principle

Notice → Talk → Connect → Seek appropriate support

Supporting a friend does not mean becoming their therapist. Listen, care and help connect them with an appropriate trusted adult or professional when needed.

EQUITY & SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Mental health is shaped by the conditions in which we live.

People do not begin with the same opportunities, resources or exposures. Poverty, discrimination, unsafe environments, violence, exclusion, education, housing and access to services can influence mental health and wellbeing.

An equity lens asks:

  • Who is more exposed to risk?
  • Who has access to protective resources?
  • Who faces barriers to support?
  • What can individuals, communities and systems change?

MEASURE

How do we measure mental health?

Researchers and health professionals use structured questionnaires and other measures to understand symptoms, wellbeing, functioning and changes over time.

These tools can support screening, research and communication. They do not automatically provide a diagnosis.

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

10 Mental Health Questions

Think carefully. These questions test understanding, application, stigma awareness and the GHE global-health perspective.

1. Which statement best describes mental health?

Correct answer: B. Mental health is a state of wellbeing that supports coping, learning, relationships and participation in life. It exists on a continuum and can change over time.

2. Which is an example of a protective factor?

Correct answer: C. Supportive relationships can provide belonging, encouragement and practical support.

3. Which statement about risk factors is most accurate?

Correct answer: A. Risk factors can increase vulnerability, but exposure to a risk factor does not determine an individual's mental health outcome.

4. Why is social connection important for mental health?

Correct answer: D. Supportive relationships can strengthen belonging, coping and access to help.

5. What is one harmful effect of mental health stigma?

Correct answer: B. Stigma can discourage people from talking about their experiences or seeking appropriate support.

6. Which question best reflects an equity approach?

Correct answer: C. Asking who faces greater exposure to risk, fewer protective resources or greater barriers to support helps identify inequities.

7. Why does GHE connect mental health from SELF to WORLD?

Correct answer: A. Mental health is influenced by interacting individual, relational, community, national and global factors.

8. What is an important limitation of a psychological questionnaire?

Correct answer: D. A questionnaire can provide useful information for screening or research, but a questionnaire alone does not automatically establish a diagnosis.

9. Which action best challenges stigma?

Correct answer: B. Learning from evidence, avoiding stereotypes, listening respectfully and treating people with dignity can help reduce stigma.

10. Which SDG is most directly connected to mental health?

Correct answer: A. SDG 3 — Good Health and Well-being. Mental health is also closely connected with SDG 10 and several other Sustainable Development Goals.
