PERSONAL CARE · PREVENT & PROTECT

Personal Safety is part of everyday health

Health includes more than preventing disease. It also means understanding our surroundings, recognizing hazards, making thoughtful decisions, and helping create environments where people can stay safer.

Return to Personal Care Hub →
WHY THIS MATTERS

Safety begins before something goes wrong

Many everyday risks can be reduced when people notice hazards, understand their environment, communicate clearly, and make decisions before a situation becomes more difficult.

01

Recognize Risk

Learn to identify situations, environments and behaviours that may increase the possibility of harm.

02

Make Informed Choices

Pause, consider the circumstances and choose actions that reduce unnecessary risk.

03

Look Out for Others

A safer community depends on communication, cooperation and respect for the wellbeing of others.

PERSONAL HINTS

Build a safety mindset

Good safety habits are usually simple. They become powerful when they are practiced consistently.

01

Pause

Before acting, take a moment to understand what is happening and what could change.

02

Notice

Pay attention to your surroundings, exits, hazards, traffic, equipment and changing conditions.

03

Communicate

Tell a trusted person when something feels unsafe or when you are uncertain about what to do.

04

Prepare

Knowing basic safety procedures and where to seek help can make it easier to respond responsibly.

LEARN

Understanding risk and prevention

Safety education helps us move from simply reacting to problems toward anticipating risks and reducing them when possible.

01

Risk is part of everyday life

Every environment contains different hazards. The goal is not to eliminate every risk but to understand and manage avoidable risks responsibly.

02

Risk depends on context

A situation that is safe under one set of conditions may become less safe when the environment, equipment, weather or circumstances change.

03

Prevention often begins with planning

Thinking ahead, understanding procedures and knowing where to get help can reduce uncertainty when circumstances change.

04

Communication is a safety tool

Speaking clearly, listening carefully and asking questions can help people identify concerns and make better decisions.

05

Asking for help is responsible

Knowing when to involve a trusted adult, emergency service or qualified professional is an important part of personal safety.

THINK

Is something actually safe?

Safety decisions require more than intuition. Ask questions, consider the evidence and look at the circumstances before deciding what to do.

01

What could go wrong?

Identify possible hazards before deciding how to proceed.

02

What can I control?

Consider which choices, behaviours or environmental changes could reduce unnecessary risk.

03

Who could be affected?

A responsible decision considers not only yourself but also other people who may be affected.

04

Where can I get reliable help?

Know which trusted people, services or authorities can provide appropriate assistance.

Personal safety becomes a global health issue when we consider the environments, systems and inequalities that influence people's ability to stay safe.

COMMUNITY

Safer Spaces

WORLD

Global Safety

Climate, conflict, displacement, inequality, infrastructure and access to services all influence safety around the world.

SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Safety is shaped by where and how we live

People do not all have the same opportunities to live, learn and travel in safe environments. Understanding these differences is an important part of health equity.

Housing

Safe, stable housing can reduce exposure to preventable environmental hazards.

Education

Safety knowledge and health literacy can influence people's ability to recognize and respond to risks.

Transportation

Safe infrastructure and accessible transportation influence how people move through their communities.

Environment

Climate, pollution, infrastructure and community design can influence everyday safety.

Access to Services

People need accessible emergency, health and community services when safety concerns arise.

Equity

Prevention strategies should consider barriers experienced by different communities.

GLOBAL HEALTH LEADERSHIP & EVIDENCE

Think like a safety leader

Good safety leadership means looking beyond individual behaviour. It asks what systems, environments and policies can make safe choices easier for everyone.

NOTICE

Identify the problem

What hazard exists? Who is exposed? What evidence tells us that the risk matters?

CHECK

Examine the evidence

Look for reliable information rather than relying only on assumptions or social-media claims.

DESIGN

Improve the environment

Ask whether the environment itself can be changed to make safer choices easier.

EQUITY

Consider everyone

A good safety solution should consider different abilities, circumstances, resources and needs.
