WHY GHBDC?

Turning Global Health Questions into Data-Driven Discovery

Health challenges around the world are increasingly understood through data. From infectious diseases and climate change to mental health, health equity, and population wellbeing, data can help us understand complex problems and identify opportunities for action.

The Global Health Big Data Challenge introduces young people to this world of discovery by connecting global health questions with data literacy, scientific reasoning, technology, creativity, and collaboration.

GHBDC is designed to help learners move beyond simply consuming information. Participants learn to ask meaningful questions, examine evidence, identify patterns, communicate findings, and consider how evidence can contribute to better decisions.

WHY NOW?

Preparing Young People for a Data-Driven World

The rapid growth of digital technologies, artificial intelligence, and health information is changing how societies understand and respond to health challenges.

Young people will increasingly encounter data in their education, workplaces, communities, and everyday lives. Developing the ability to interpret evidence critically and use technology responsibly is therefore an essential component of future global health leadership.

Data Literacy

Understanding, interpreting, questioning, and communicating data.

Critical Thinking

Moving from information to evidence-based questions and conclusions.

Responsible Innovation

Exploring technology and AI with ethics, responsibility, and equity in mind.

THE GHBDC JOURNEY

From Curiosity to Discovery

GHBDC guides learners through a progressive journey that combines global health exploration, data, scientific reasoning, creativity, and communication.

01

Discover

Explore a real-world global health question and understand why it matters to people and communities.

02

Understand Data

Learn where data come from, what they represent, and how data can help us understand health challenges.

03

Analyze

Identify patterns, compare information, ask questions, and develop evidence-informed interpretations.

04

Innovate

Think creatively about possible solutions, using science, technology, collaboration, and responsible innovation.

05

Communicate

Present findings clearly through visualizations, stories, presentations, or other appropriate forms of scientific communication.

CHALLENGE DOMAINS

Global Health Questions Worth Exploring

GHBDC can evolve with emerging global health priorities, allowing learners to investigate diverse questions through data and evidence.

Health Equity

Exploring differences in health outcomes and the social, economic, and environmental factors that shape them.

Climate & Health

Investigating how climate change and environmental conditions influence human health and wellbeing.

Pandemic Preparedness

Understanding infectious disease patterns, preparedness, prevention, and the importance of timely evidence.

Mental Health

Exploring population mental health, wellbeing, resilience, and the factors that influence healthy communities.

Healthy Communities

Examining how communities, environments, policies, and health systems contribute to wellbeing.

Sustainable Development

Connecting health with the Sustainable Development Goals and the broader determinants of human development.

DATA, AI & ETHICS

Learning to Use Data Responsibly

GHE recognizes that data and artificial intelligence can create extraordinary opportunities for discovery, but also raise important questions about privacy, fairness, bias, representation, accountability, and equity.

GHBDC therefore aims to introduce responsible data and AI thinking from the beginning: learners are encouraged not only to ask what the data show, but also to ask how the data were generated, whose voices may be missing, what assumptions

WHAT LEARNERS DEVELOP

Skills for the Next Generation

GHBDC is designed to develop transferable skills that extend beyond the challenge itself and support lifelong learning, scientific inquiry, and responsible leadership.

Data Literacy

Reading, interpreting, questioning, and communicating information from data.

Critical Thinking

Evaluating evidence, identifying assumptions, and developing thoughtful conclusions.

Research Skills

Asking meaningful questions, finding evidence, and exploring complex health problems.

Collaboration

Working across disciplines, backgrounds, communities, and perspectives.

Scientific Communication

Turning evidence and analysis into clear, accessible, and compelling communication.

Ethical Innovation

Considering equity, responsibility, and potential consequences when developing new ideas.

BUILDING TOGETHER

A Collaborative Platform for Youth Innovation

GHBDC is envisioned as a collaborative initiative connecting young people with educators, scientists, universities, public health institutions, technology partners, and organizations committed to youth development.

Through partnerships, GHBDC can bring authentic datasets, mentorship, expertise, learning opportunities, and new pathways for young people to contribute to global health.

Schools Universities STEM Organizations Public Health Institutions Technology Partners Youth Organizations

LOOKING AHEAD

From Data Literacy to Global Health Leadership

The long-term vision of GHBDC is to create a growing community of young people who are comfortable asking difficult questions, working with evidence, embracing diverse perspectives, and imagining better solutions for the health of people and the planet.

As the initiative grows, GHBDC can evolve into a global platform for youth learning, collaboration, mentorship, innovation, and leadership.
