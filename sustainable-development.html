Sustainable Development Is About Our Shared Future

The United Nations 2030 Agenda describes sustainable development as an integrated agenda for people, planet and prosperity, supported by peace and partnership. The 17 Goals are interconnected rather than separate projects.

01
👥

People

People need health, food, education, safety, dignity, opportunity and the ability to participate in decisions that affect their lives.

02
🌍

Planet

Healthy societies depend on functioning ecosystems, clean air and water, stable climates and responsible use of natural resources.

03
📈

Prosperity

Sustainable prosperity means creating opportunities while considering whether economic activity is inclusive, resilient and compatible with a healthy planet.

04
🕊️

Peace

Peaceful and inclusive societies require justice, trustworthy institutions, participation and respect for human rights.

05
🤝

Partnership

No country, community or institution can solve every global challenge alone. Collaboration turns shared knowledge into shared action.

THINK DEEPLY

What Does “Sustainable” Really Mean?

Sustainable development is not simply about planting trees, building new technologies or growing an economy. It is about considering the consequences of decisions across time, sectors and populations.

A solution that improves one outcome while creating serious problems somewhere else may not be sustainable. Systems thinking helps us look beyond the immediate result.

GHE question: “Better for whom, better for what, and better for how long?”

THE GLOBAL FRAMEWORK

The 17 Sustainable Development Goals

The SDGs provide a common framework for addressing poverty, hunger, health, education, inequality, environmental protection, peace and partnership.

The 17 Sustainable Development Goals of the United Nations 2030 Agenda.
01

No Poverty

Poverty shapes access to food, housing, education, health and opportunity.

02

Zero Hunger

Food security, nutrition and sustainable agriculture influence lifelong health.

03

Good Health & Well-being

Health is both a goal in itself and a foundation for human development.

04

Quality Education

Education builds knowledge, opportunity, agency and human capital.

05

Gender Equality

Equality and empowerment affect health, education, safety, income and participation.

06

Clean Water & Sanitation

Safe water and sanitation are fundamental to health, dignity and resilient communities.

07

Affordable & Clean Energy

Energy access affects households, health, education, productivity and climate.

08

Decent Work & Economic Growth

Work, income, safety and social protection shape living conditions.

09

Industry, Innovation & Infrastructure

Resilient infrastructure and responsible innovation can expand access and opportunity.

10

Reduced Inequalities

Development is stronger when opportunities and resources are not systematically restricted.

11

Sustainable Cities

Housing, transport, air quality, safety and planning influence urban health.

12

Responsible Consumption

What we produce, purchase, use and discard affects health, resources and ecosystems.

13

Climate Action

Climate change affects health, food, water, livelihoods, migration and ecosystems.

14

Life Below Water

Healthy oceans support food systems, livelihoods, biodiversity and planetary health.

15

Life on Land

Forests, soils, biodiversity and ecosystems provide essential services for human life.

16

Peace, Justice & Strong Institutions

Trust, accountability, justice and effective institutions support sustainable development.

17

Partnerships for the Goals

Knowledge, financing, technology and cooperation help turn shared goals into action.

The SDGs are designed to be understood together. A change in one part of a system can create effects somewhere else.

HEALTH IS EVERYWHERE

SDG 3 Is the Health Goal — But Health Is Not Only SDG 3

Health is influenced by the conditions in which people are born, grow, learn, work, live and age. This means that progress on education, poverty, food, water, housing, gender equality, climate, cities and institutions can also influence health.

Education → Opportunity → Living Conditions → Health

WHO's SDG monitoring work emphasizes the interconnected nature of health and the wider Sustainable Development Goals.

DEVELOPMENT & HUMAN CAPITAL

Development Means Investing in People

Sustainable development is not only about economic output. Human capital — including people's health, knowledge and skills — influences opportunity, resilience and long-term prosperity.

01

Health

Healthy people are better able to learn, participate and adapt to change.

02

Education

Knowledge and skills expand individual and community capacity.

03

Opportunity

Decent work and social protection can influence resilience and living conditions.

04

Resilience

Strong people and systems are better positioned to withstand shocks.

05

Planet

Development must account for environmental and climate risks.

Think Like a Systems Explorer

SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Sustainable Development Starts With Conditions

The conditions in which people live and the resources available to them influence health and well-being. Sustainable development therefore requires attention to social, economic and environmental determinants.

01

Local

What is happening in a neighbourhood?

  • Housing
  • Food access
  • Water & sanitation
  • Transportation
  • Safety & green space
02

National

What systems and policies shape opportunity?

  • Education
  • Health systems
  • Social protection
  • Employment
  • Public policy
03

Global

What forces cross borders?

  • Climate change
  • Migration
  • Trade
  • Conflict
  • Global cooperation
GHE Data Principle

Do not simply ask whether a population is doing well. Ask whether measurable differences exist, where they occur, what may explain them, and whether those differences are changing over time.
