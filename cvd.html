01 • WHY IS THIS IMPORTANT?

Heart health is a global health issue

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are diseases affecting the heart and blood vessels. They include conditions such as coronary heart disease, cerebrovascular disease and other disorders of the circulatory system.

They remain the leading cause of death globally. WHO estimates that about 19.8 million people died from cardiovascular diseases in 2022. More than three quarters of these deaths occurred in low- and middle-income countries.

Heart attacks and strokes are especially important because they can occur suddenly, while the conditions that increase their risk may develop silently over many years.

19.8M CVD deaths in 2022
85% of CVD deaths were due to heart attack and stroke
>75% of CVD deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries

03 • EXPLORE

Meet the cardiovascular system

❤️

The heart

A muscular organ that pumps blood through the body, delivering oxygen and nutrients and helping remove waste products.

🩸

Blood vessels

Arteries carry blood away from the heart, while veins return blood toward the heart. Tiny vessels called capillaries connect the two.

〰️

Blood pressure

Blood pressure is the force of blood against the walls of blood vessels. Persistently high blood pressure is called hypertension.

🧠

Stroke

A stroke occurs when blood flow to part of the brain is interrupted or when a blood vessel in the brain bleeds.

04 • LEARN

What science tells us

Hypertension: a risk that can remain invisible

Hypertension means that blood pressure is persistently too high. Many people with hypertension do not feel any symptoms, which is why blood pressure measurement is important.

WHO estimated that 1.4 billion adults aged 30–79 years worldwide had hypertension in 2024. About 44% were estimated to be unaware that they had the condition.

Important idea: You cannot reliably know whether someone has hypertension simply by looking at them or asking how they feel. Blood pressure needs to be measured.

Risk factors are not all individual choices

Cardiovascular risk can be influenced by tobacco exposure, physical inactivity, unhealthy diets, high salt intake, harmful alcohol use, high blood pressure, diabetes, abnormal blood lipids, obesity and air pollution.

But these risks do not exist in a vacuum. The food available in a neighbourhood, the price of healthy foods, transportation systems, opportunities for physical activity, working conditions, housing, education, income and access to healthcare can all shape exposure and opportunity.

07 • SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Health is shaped by the conditions around us

Social determinants of health are the conditions in which people are born, grow, learn, work, live and age.

💰 Income & economic security

Income can affect food choices, housing, transportation, recreation, healthcare access and the ability to manage chronic conditions.

📚 Education & health literacy

Education can influence access to health information, understanding of risk and the ability to navigate health systems.

🏘️ Housing & neighbourhoods

Safe housing, walkable communities, green spaces and clean air can create healthier conditions.

🏥 Healthcare access

Prevention depends on affordable, available and acceptable primary healthcare, medicines, diagnostic services and follow-up.

🌫️ Environmental conditions

Air pollution and other environmental exposures can contribute to cardiovascular risk.

⚖️ Equity

A healthy society does not simply ask everyone to make the same choices. It works to ensure that everyone has meaningful opportunities to be healthy.

09 • GLOBAL HEALTH LEADERSHIP & EVIDENCE

Who helps the world respond?

Global health is a team effort. Different organizations have different responsibilities.

WHO

Develops global guidance, standards, surveillance systems and technical tools for cardiovascular disease and NCD prevention.

WHO HEARTS

Supports countries in strengthening cardiovascular disease prevention and hypertension control through primary healthcare.

UNDP

Supports policy and development approaches that address broader determinants and governance of NCDs.

World Bank

Generates evidence and supports health-system and development investments relevant to NCD prevention and universal health coverage.

IHME

Produces global and national estimates that help researchers and decision-makers understand disease burden and trends.

National & Local Health Authorities

Translate evidence into policies, prevention programs, primary care, regulation and services within communities.
